Doe v. Black: Civil Suit Reportedly Dismissed With Prejudice After Plaintiff’s Request

Independent reporting on October 16 and a statement issued that day by Leon Black’s counsel describe the dismissal with prejudice of Doe v. Black, a federal civil suit filed in July 2023. The plaintiff requested dismissal in a document reportedly submitted October 12, saying health considerations prevented her from continuing. She expressly said the request was not a retraction of her allegations. Black denies the allegations.

Source-status boundary: GAH has verified the dated October 16 statement by defense counsel and independent October 16 reporting. As of this publication, GAH has not independently inspected the signed October dismissal order. Its ECF number, exact filing or entry date, and procedural wording are open verification targets. The defense announcement is a party statement, not a substitute for the signed order.

What “with prejudice” means—and what it does not

A dismissal with prejudice generally ends the plaintiff’s ability to bring the same claims again in a new lawsuit, subject to any available appellate or extraordinary procedural relief. It is a significant case outcome, not a jury finding that the events alleged happened or did not happen. It does not constitute a criminal acquittal or independently settle separate congressional inquiries or financial questions involving Epstein.

According to the October 16 report, Black did not oppose the requested dismissal. His lawyer stated that there had been no settlement or other inducement from Black; that is counsel’s representation, not a finding GAH independently verified.

The competing positions

Plaintiff: The reported October 12 request attributes withdrawal to the effects of continued litigation and explicitly preserves her position on the factual allegations. Black and counsel: Black denies the accusations; counsel maintains the case was unfounded and said they were pleased by the reported dismissal. Court record: The earlier April 23 signed order contains express findings about litigation misconduct and expressly rejected case-terminating sanctions at that stage. The later dismissal order still needs direct inspection for its precise legal language.
