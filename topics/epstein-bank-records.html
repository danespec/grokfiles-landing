Epstein Bank Records: SARs, Wires and Accounts

Find financial records by evidence class and distinguish transaction totals, reporting obligations, regulatory findings and allegations.

Scope: This is an editorial research guide linking previously published GAH analyses and official source context. Related reading is not independent corroboration. No new primary-source authentication or finding of misconduct is claimed.

Why different bank totals cannot be added together

A wire count is not a count of unique victims or criminal transactions. The JPMorgan reporting-gap investigation and 4,725-wire explainer describe different scopes in the published financial record. Compare reporting period, institutions, directions of transfers and the relevant account universe before combining figures. A suspicious activity report is a report of concern, not a verdict or an independent proof that each listed payment was criminal.

What a regulator actually found

The July 2020 New York financial regulator consent order describes Deutsche Bank compliance failures in its Epstein relationship. Those regulatory findings are distinct from the separate question of the purpose of any particular transaction. The banker interview source audit examines another evidence type: interview accounts. Neither an interview summary nor a congressional request is interchangeable with a bank ledger.

Read the account-level receipts

The Amador forensic report guide (EFTA02810827) separates an expert’s analysis from a court determination. The Gratitude America money trail shows why internal transfers must not be counted as grants. The exhibit (EFTA00080260) and financial-intelligence inquiry (EFTA00153569) are different source objects; an inquiry does not furnish the underlying missing bank statements.

Research the legal and oversight trail

The Senate Finance Committee’s March 2025 statement describes findings from its review of financial records. Attribute those findings to the committee rather than presenting every investigative question as adjudicated. Atlas bank litigation and settlements and Staley correspondence provide related chronologies, not an assertion that all accounts or cases overlap. Keep the payment, regulatory, litigation and settlement records in separate columns.
