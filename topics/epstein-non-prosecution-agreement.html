Epstein Non-Prosecution Agreement: 2007–2008

Follow the decision record from the proposed federal charges through the non-prosecution agreement and the subsequent review of victim notification.

Scope: This is an editorial research guide linking previously published GAH analyses and official source context. Related reading is not independent corroboration. No new primary-source authentication or finding of misconduct is claimed.

What did the agreement resolve?

The November 2020 DOJ statement on the Office of Professional Responsibility review describes the federal investigation of 2006–2008 and its resolution through a non-prosecution agreement. OPR concluded that former U.S. Attorney Alexander Acosta exercised poor judgment, while not finding professional misconduct by Department attorneys. Those are distinct conclusions. A later review of the agreement is not the agreement itself and is not evidence that every unresolved allegation about its negotiation is true.

The draft indictment and the decision sequence

Start with the draft federal indictment guide and EFTA01703108. A proposed charging document is not a filed indictment. Then read the July 2007 decision-gap reconstruction and the December decision-gap investigation. These are separate temporal checkpoints rather than a single explanation for the federal resolution. The later state proceedings must also be kept distinct from a federal trial on the draft counts.

The victim-notification record

EFTA00013764 documents an instruction to hold prepared letters on December 7, 2007. The Hold the Letter investigation distinguishes the actual instruction from unanswered questions about the consultation preceding it. The defense-access and notification investigation adds the surrounding record. An instruction does not by itself prove why it was issued or identify an unnamed official.

Read the later case separately

The July 8, 2019 SDNY announcement concerns a distinct federal indictment. The Atlas tracks the Palm Beach/NPA chronology, the OPR review and the victim-rights litigation as separate chains. Readers comparing allegations, decisions and court outcomes should record the issuing authority and the date of each artifact, rather than substituting later conclusions for the earlier source.
