Deutsche Bank and Epstein: Interviews, Bank Exhibits and Open Records

The public record has several distinct Deutsche Bank evidence lanes: released FBI interview reports, a 2020 investigative email, and a bank-derived exhibit used in a financial reconstruction. They should not be treated as one continuous or proven account.

Evidence boundary: An organization appearing in these records is not evidence of guilt. Every entry identifies its source and separates what the document supports from what remains unknown. This page does not identify protected victims or witnesses.

Documentary timeline and source trail

  1. Released interview records

    Three Deutsche-linked FBI FD-302 reports were reviewed in the published investigation. They memorialize interviewees’ statements rather than FBI conclusions.

  2. August 2020

  3. September 2026

    A Senate Finance inquiry described a separate unreleased banker FD-302 without publicly identifying the banker or the bank. The identity must remain unresolved.

    Source: Source-limited missing-record analysis

  4. 2015–2019 accounting

    EFTA00080260 is a bank-derived exhibit following one Gratitude America account. It does not represent the entirety of that charity’s accounts or banking relationships.

    Source: Account exhibit

What these records do not establish

  • Interviews record competing or incomplete accounts; an FD-302 is not an institutional finding.
  • The 2020 prospective-proffer email is not proof of a completed interview, an FD-302, or the identity of the undisclosed banker.
  • The accounting exhibit follows one identified account; its totals cannot be generalized to all Deutsche Bank activity.
  • A reported compliance concern or banking relationship alone does not establish criminal misconduct.
