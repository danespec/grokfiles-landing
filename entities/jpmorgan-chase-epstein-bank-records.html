JPMorgan Chase and Epstein: Nine SARs and the Reporting Timeline

The reviewed record concerns the timing and scope of suspicious-activity reporting associated with Jeffrey Epstein’s JPMorgan accounts. This dossier separates the nine-report chronology, one narrower wire-transfer count, the bank’s response, and unanswered oversight questions.

Evidence boundary: An organization appearing in these records is not evidence of guilt. Every entry identifies its source and separates what the document supports from what remains unknown. This page does not identify protected victims or witnesses.

Documentary timeline and source trail

  1. 2002–2016

    Seven suspicious-activity reports filed before 2019 covered a combined $4,316,424, according to the November 2025 Senate Finance staff memorandum.

    Source: Senate Finance staff memorandum

  2. August–September 2019

    Two later reports, filed after Epstein’s July 2019 arrest, covered $1,282,799,188 of historical activity; their reporting dates must not be confused with the activity dates.

    Source: Nine-SAR chronology

  3. September 24, 2025

    A separate Senate Finance letter describes 4,725 wires totaling approximately $1.1 billion in a 2019 filing. This is not the total of all nine reports.

    Source: Senate Finance letter

  4. 2023 forensic report

    EFTA02810827 is cited by the Senate memorandum as an expert forensic-accounting report; it is not itself an adjudication of criminal liability.

    Source: GAH source review

What these records do not establish

  • A suspicious-activity report is a regulatory filing, not a conviction or proof that every transaction was illegal.
  • The nine-report totals and the 4,725-wire figure describe different scopes. Do not add or interchange them.
  • The Senate memorandum summarizes allegations and records, and records a JPMorgan response contesting an implication of knowing facilitation. Those positions are distinguished in the linked investigation.
  • The public sources do not establish a complete customer-ledger reconciliation or individual criminal intent for each listed transfer.
