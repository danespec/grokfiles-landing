Did the DOJ Permanently Delete the Epstein Photo File EFTA00000468?

The file disappeared in December 2025 and was restored. The renewed September 2026 deletion claim requires evidence the public record has not independently established.

GAH Research Desk · October 19, 2026 · Reinterprets earlier source audits; no new underlying documents claimed

The short answer

A temporary removal on December 20, 2025 and restoration on December 21 are documented. A permanent deletion or second September 2026 removal has not been independently established by GAH.

What the records show

  • Contemporaneous reporting and archived listing evidence document that DOJ Dataset 1 initially included EFTA00000468 and that the file became inaccessible or disappeared from its listing on December 20.
  • DOJ subsequently attributed its temporary removal to a victim-safety review and announced restoration on December 21.
  • The GAH October 14 retrieval attempt hit an age-verification gate on this file and neighboring control files—not a target-specific missing-PDF response.

What they do not prove

  • GAH did not obtain both the original and restored PDF bytes to confirm DOJ’s assertion that no changes were made.
  • A September 28–29, 2026 second-removal claim lacks a preserved, independently repeatable file-specific response and listing comparison in the GAH audit.
  • A photograph containing a public figure is not proof of wrongdoing or criminal knowledge.

Why the distinction matters

“Removed,” “restored,” and “missing from a crawler response” describe different states. When neighboring DOJ URLs all redirect to age verification, that control result cannot distinguish file removal from access gating. An archived screenshot establishes the historical listing change, not an undisclosed motive.

The missing receipt

A dated, reproducible DOJ listing comparison, the full redirect chain and an age-verified PDF download with checksums would clarify current availability and any alleged replacement. Evidence of a secret motive would require more than access logs.
