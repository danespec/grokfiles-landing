Keep the publication dispute separate from the document record

The publication listing answers a limited but important factual question: an interview was listed as released. That is enough to correct the earlier caption’s statement that it never aired. It is not enough to authenticate the full production history, determine whether anything was edited, or decide which participant’s account of an alleged naming condition is accurate. Those questions need the complete original statements and production records, not the mere existence of a listing.

The older Epstein email occupies a different evidentiary lane. It records a statement about a prospective Carbyne meeting, without confirming that the meeting occurred. Moving from that statement to an explanation of a 2026 editorial choice would require an intervening documentary connection. Financing announcements and investment disclosures provide context but cannot supply that missing connection. Readers should therefore compare the two attributed accounts on their own terms and keep the separate historical record attached to its date and stated limits.
