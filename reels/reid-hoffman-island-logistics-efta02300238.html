Read the document behind the reel

The November 28 email addresses Hoffman and Ito and relays the pilot’s information for Sunday’s island-to-Bedford route. It requests customs information and gives an expected arrival time.

The document shows that the office was coordinating travel. A request for addresses and an expected arrival are different evidence from a signed passenger manifest or proof that a flight completed. The source note does not republish the requested private addresses.

What this does not prove

This email records departure arrangements. It does not itself prove that the flight operated or identify everyone who boarded. The earlier Vancouver itinerary and 2019 apology in the original caption are separate attributed claims whose underlying primary records are not supplied by EFTA02300238. A logistics email does not establish criminal conduct.

Open questions and records still needed

  • A flight movement, manifest or arrival record for the proposed November 30 route.
  • The earlier Vancouver itinerary and original 2019 statement, before independently treating those caption claims as verified findings.

    Reading this particular record

    The request for customs information tells readers which administrative step the sender was trying to complete. It does not establish that the requested addresses were returned, that clearance was granted or that every intended passenger travelled. Similarly, an expected arrival time is a forecast within the arrangements, rather than an observation of an arrival. The November 28 message and proposed November 30 movement must retain their own dates. They should not be relabelled as evidence about a December Barak trip simply because earlier site templates grouped these identifiers together. Passenger-level confirmation would require a record of the operated flight.
