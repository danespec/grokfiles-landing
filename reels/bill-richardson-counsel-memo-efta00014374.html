Bill Richardson: EFTA00014374 and the limits of the counsel-call memo

Bill Richardson: counsel memo and proposed callback. Read EFTA00014374, EFTA02662212 with GAH’s original caption, receipts, denials where relevant and source limits.

GrokArchiveHub · Source notes published October 25, 2026

Read the document behind the reel

The prosecutor’s note records a discussion with counsel about an allegation and the investigation’s position at that time. Its language about possible later inquiries and an attorney proffer matters alongside the decision not to seek an interview then.

A different document records a proposed callback to Epstein. It supplies evidence of a communication channel and an intention to call; it does not independently establish a completed conversation. Richardson’s reported denial remains beside the attributed allegation.

Bill Richardson Jeffrey Epstein — EFTA00014374, the prosecutors' own memo. August 18, 2019: an SDNY prosecutor writes up a call with Bill Richardson's counsel about the allegation unsealed days earlier in the Giuffre civil papers. The memo's read of that deposition: Richardson "was ordered to, but doesn't appear to say that anything actually happened." And: prosecutors did not intend to subpoena him or request an interview — "there are not specific allegations that we need to hear his side of at this time." The releases do document a phone channel — in January 2017 Epstein's scheduler relayed that Richardson would call Epstein in ten minutes (EFTA02662212). Contact is documented. The allegation itself remained an allegation: Richardson denied it, he was never charged, and he died in 2023. 869 documents in the DOJ releases name Richardson — most are duplicated tips and news compilations. Document receipts and their limits

  1. DOJ EFTA00014374 — publisher-hosted released PDF

It also requests a possible attorney proffer and leaves open later inquiries. These are the memo writer’s contemporaneous characterizations, not a court’s findings. What this does not prove

The allegation is attributed in the prosecutor’s memo, not established as fact. The original GAH caption carries Richardson’s denial and reports that he was never charged. The memo’s no-interview position is expressly time-bounded and is not an exoneration or a permanent immunity decision. The proposed callback does not establish that contact actually occurred. Historical search counts and their composition are attributed to the original caption, not freshly audited here.

Open questions and records still needed

  • Any subsequent attorney proffer or later prosecutorial decision before extrapolating from the August 18 memo.
  • Call records or follow-up correspondence establishing whether the proposed callback happened.
