Read the document behind the reel

The released artifact is dated December 19, 2019. Its Wexner section describes a July 25 meeting with counsel; the meeting date and the memo date should not be conflated. The relevant passage is on printed pages 65–66, not the file’s opening page.

The financial-control and misappropriation account comes from Wexner’s lawyers as summarized by prosecutors. The same passage records their denial that he knew about Epstein’s abuse. The page carries both parts with the same attribution.

The memo describes a 1991 power of attorney but does not supply the executed instrument in this passage. The caption’s wider negative-search findings are preserved as attributed claims, without asserting that a fresh exhaustive search proved their absence.

What this does not prove

The passage is prosecutors’ summary of Wexner’s lawyers’ account, not sworn testimony, underlying bank records, the executed power of attorney or an adjudicated accounting. Counsel denied Wexner knew about Epstein’s inappropriate or unlawful activity with young women and characterized the relationship as more professional than social; those are attributed positions, not findings adopted here. The misappropriation and wealth explanations likewise remain attributed. The reel’s claims that the power of attorney is absent across the releases and that there is no Wexner-authored document are historical scoped search findings, not independently re-run corpus-wide conclusions. This page does not establish wrongdoing by Wexner.

Open questions and records still needed

  • The executed 1991 power of attorney, amendments and revocation records.
  • Transaction-level records for the transfers, compensation and described settlement, before treating counsel’s account as an independently reconciled financial history.
  • A reproducible search log to audit the original caption’s absence claims and document counts.
