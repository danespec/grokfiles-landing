Leon Black’s $158 million: EFTA02730996 and the Senate questions

The original GAH Leon Black reel, Dechert’s $158 million review and Wyden’s EFTA02731023 letter, with their different evidentiary limits.

GrokArchiveHub · Source notes published October 24, 2026

Read the document behind the reel

This reel joins two dated source types: a commissioned corporate review’s compensation total and a later Senate chairman’s questions about how those payments were calculated and substantiated. One is not a court validation of the other.

The original reel discusses $158 million for work in 2012–2017. The related evidence brief separately examines the later Senate total of $170 million; the difference requires a reconciliation rather than silently changing the reel’s historical claim.

The caption’s 9,131-document count is a reported search-scope figure, not a count of meetings, payments, independent events or criminal acts. The underlying review’s conclusions and limitations remain attached to the source notes.

Leon Black Jeffrey Epstein — EFTA02730996, the Dechert memo. January 22, 2021: Dechert LLP's independent investigation for Apollo's board, filed publicly with the SEC, puts the number in writing: "Black compensated Epstein for his work in amounts that were intended to be proportional to the value provided by Epstein. Those payments for work performed over the period 2012 through 2017 totaled $158 million." Itemized: $50M in 2013, $70M in 2014, $30M in 2015, a final $8M in April 2017 — paid to Epstein's Southern Trust Company — plus $30.5M in 2017 loans from Black's BV70 LLC. July 24, 2023: Senate Finance Chairman Ron Wyden writes to Black (EFTA02731023): "you have refused to answer questions or provide documents related to payments you made to Epstein or substantiate how such payments were calculated or were compensation for services." (The later House subpoena fight is a separate proceeding — this reel stays on the Senate record.) The gate, stated plainly: Dechert recorded the payments as compensation for tax and estate work and found no evidence tying them to Epstein's criminal activity. A payment record is not a crime finding. The documented fact is $158 million — and a Senate chairman on record that the calculation behind it was never substantiated. 9,131 documents in the DOJ releases name Black. What this does not prove

The payment record does not establish a crime. Dechert described tax and estate work and said it found no evidence tying the payments to Epstein’s criminal activity; it was a commissioned review, not a court verdict. The 2023 Senate questions are not adjudicated findings. The later $170 million Senate total is a different scope from the review’s $158 million and must not be silently substituted.

Open questions and records still needed

  • A transaction-level reconciliation of the later $170 million figure against Dechert’s $158 million total.
  • The written agreements, calculation methodology and underlying deliverables requested by Senate Finance.
  • Independent records that support or challenge the commissioned review’s explanation of the compensation.
