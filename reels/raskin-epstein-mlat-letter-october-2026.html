Original published Instagram caption

Open the original GAH reel · Instagram publication: 2026-10-24

Jamie Raskin Jeffrey Epstein DOJ — the October 21 letter, read from the document itself. On October 21, 2026, Rep. Jamie Raskin, the top Democrat on the House Judiciary Committee, wrote to Attorney General Todd Blanche and Vaughn Ary, director of DOJ's Office of International Affairs. The four-page letter makes two demands, quoted verbatim in this reel: 1. Every record of foreign Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty requests for DOJ help investigating potential Epstein co-conspirators. 2. Every record of DOJ's decisions to deny or oppose cooperation requests from state and local investigators. Raskin alleges — his word is "obstructing" — that DOJ is blocking Britain's investigation into Peter Mandelson and New Mexico's Zorro Ranch investigation. New Mexico AG Raúl Torrez says his office's request for the unredacted files was "stonewalled"; the state says it eventually received 31 pages, much of it news clippings. New Mexico sued DOJ over the files in August; DOJ moved to dismiss in October, saying victim privacy and court orders limit what it can share. No DOJ response to this letter itself has been located yet. It is an oversight demand — not a subpoena, not a charge, not a finding. The deadline in the letter: 5:00 p.m., November 4, 2026. EpsteinWiki has a knowledge-base page on the inquiry. Document receipts and their limits

  1. Signed House Judiciary letter, October 21, 2026 — official congressional PDF

    Four-page signed October 21, 2026 letter to Blanche and Ary. Page 4 requests records about foreign MLAT assistance and decisions to deny or oppose state/local cooperation, with a November 4, 2026, 5 pm response deadline. The document does not specify a time zone. It is an oversight request, not a subpoena or court finding.

Leak IDs identify a separate mailbox source family. They are labeled rather than assigned invented public document URLs. An archive locator is not itself authentication of every statement in a document.

What this does not prove

Raskin’s obstruction language is his allegation, not a judicial finding. The letter does not prove that DOJ received, agreed to or complied with the request, or that every dispute it describes is established. The New Mexico records lawsuit is separate from its executors case; DOJ’s stated privacy and court-order defenses are carried in the existing report. The original caption’s no-response statement is historical, not a fresh exhaustive search certification. The caption condenses the requests; this page does not certify the audio as a verbatim transcription.

Open questions and records still needed

  • A publicly authenticated DOJ response, production record or subsequent congressional action.
  • The underlying state/DOJ pleadings and foreign requests, rather than treating the letter as adjudication of those disputes.
