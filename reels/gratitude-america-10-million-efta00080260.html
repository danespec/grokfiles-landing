Gratitude America’s $10 million: EFTA00080260 and the account trail

The GAH reel behind the $10 million deposit, $8.45 million in internal transfers and $325,000 in 2016 external payments.

GrokArchiveHub · Source notes published October 24, 2026

Read the document behind the reel

The reel’s most important distinction is classification: $8.45 million of the tracked 2016 outflows moved to other accounts of Gratitude America, while $325,000 went to outside recipients. Calling every debit a charitable grant would change the meaning of the record.

Those numbers come from one account schedule. They cannot establish how much the entire charity gave across every account or year. The full investigation’s 28-row ledger keeps dates, destinations and source-page references visible.

The later correspondence records a foreign financial-intelligence inquiry and a forensic-review reference. The underlying SAR narratives and accountant’s report remain gaps; a reference to scrutiny is not a finding of criminal conduct.

$10 million went into the charity. Outside charities got $325,000. October 14, 2015: BV70 LLC credited $10 million to Gratitude America Ltd.'s Deutsche Bank money-market account (EFTA00080260, Exhibit A). In 2016, $8.775 million flowed back out — but reclassification matters: $8.45 million went to other accounts held by the same charity, not grants. Only $325,000 reached outside recipients ($100K Moskowitz Foundation; $225K Melanoma Research Alliance). Later records: France's TRACFIN flagged a €50,000 wire, three SARs were referenced, and an IRS forensic accountant reviewed the accounts (EFTA00153569–571, EFTA00128841). Suspicion is not a finding — none of these documents, by themselves, establish a crime. Dechert's SEC-filed review separately identifies Leon Black as the donor of a $10 million October 2015 donation. Leak IDs identify a separate mailbox source family. They are labeled rather than assigned invented public document URLs. An archive locator is not itself authentication of every statement in a document.

What this does not prove

The $325,000 figure concerns external payments from the tracked account in 2016, not every grant made by the charity across all accounts and years. Transfers between the charity’s own accounts are not outside grants. A financial-intelligence inquiry, referenced SAR or forensic-review form does not itself establish a crime. Dechert’s donor attribution is a separate source from the bank-derived exhibit.

Open questions and records still needed

  • The receiving-account records needed to follow the $8.45 million beyond the tracked money-market account.
  • The underlying SAR narratives, French inquiry attachments and forensic accountant’s report referenced in released correspondence.
  • A complete account-by-account and year-by-year reconciliation before making charity-wide spending claims.
