    Reading this particular record

    There are two separate chronology problems here. Nested timestamps describe the order of messages in an email chain; they do not automatically identify a journey’s departure and arrival. A question about recommended dates likewise establishes discussion of possible plans without documenting their completion. The relevant follow-up would be a record tied to an actual movement, such as an identified manifest or arrival record, rather than a second retelling of the same invitation. Until such a connection is supplied, this correspondence belongs in the planning record. It should not be counted as a confirmed visit merely because the proposed destination is mentioned.

    Keep the proposed visit separate from the reported response

    The December exchange contains a question, an availability answer and a follow-up about a recommended day or night. Those are three parts of a planning conversation, rather than three independent confirmations of travel. The original caption also attributes a public statement about declining a visit to Musk. That later statement should retain its attribution and should not be treated as a passenger-level record for a particular date. Comparing the two accounts requires attention to the proposed holiday period and the scope of the reported denial; neither can silently fill the missing arrival evidence.
