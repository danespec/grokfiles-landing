The June 2020 email describes an expected legal argument about a U.S. witness-interview request and UK voluntary-cooperation guidance. It supplies a procedural paper trail, not proof that litigation was filed.

The original reel contrasts that trail with later warrant reporting. The parallel is editorial comparison, not one continuous proceeding, a prediction, obstruction finding or a conclusion about criminal liability.

Six years before the warrants were quashed, U.S. officials saw the legal playbook coming. A June 18, 2020 DOJ correspondence records UK officials anticipating Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor's lawyers challenging a mutual legal assistance request by judicial review — a separate proceeding from the 2026 search warrants. Document receipts and their limits

  1. DOJ EFTA00088020

    June 18, 2020 DOJ email chain records UK officials anticipating that Blackfords might seek judicial review of Home Office approval of a mutual legal assistance request. An anticipated filing is not a docket entry proving it happened.

  2. DOJ EFTA00022062 — publisher-hosted released PDF

    April 3, 2020 DOJ assistance request seeks an interview and related records. It describes Andrew as a witness, not then a target, in the Epstein and separate Nygard investigations. That status is time-bounded. Linked as a publisher-hosted EFTA-stamped released PDF mirror, not byte-authenticated against DOJ.

  3. Official High Court order and reasons — October 8, 2026 — official Judiciary PDF; text checked October 25

    Official October 8, 2026 order and reasons, 10 PDF pages, AC 2026-LON-002321. Paragraphs 4–6 explain the unlawful warrants and pending retention application. Paragraph 10 says the judge approved allowing the claim on July 22 but the order remained unsealed. Paragraphs 11–19 explain the publicity/access dispute.

Leak IDs identify a separate mailbox source family. They are labeled rather than assigned invented public document URLs. An archive locator is not itself authentication of every statement in a document.

What this does not prove

The 2020 request and 2026 warrant proceeding are different matters. The correspondence proves neither that the expected 2020 challenge was filed nor that officials predicted the later outcome. The official October 8 order is now checked; it does not resolve every underlying court-file question or establish any later retention outcome. Andrew’s denial remains carried in the existing investigation. The warrant ruling is not a finding of guilt and did not end the investigation.

Open questions and records still needed

  • The 2020 docket, if any, for the anticipated MLA challenge.
  • The underlying consent order, statement of reasons and later retention decisions, separate from the verified October 8 order.
