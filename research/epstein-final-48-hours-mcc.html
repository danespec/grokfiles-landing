Epstein Death Timeline — Direct Answers

What happened during Epstein's final 48 hours?

Archive-native sequence: Aug. 9 evening movement pointers (EFTA00039416), overnight CH0080 file manifest, Aug. 10 discovery reporting (EFTA00039025/OIG), with EMS timing and exact discovery clock marked SOURCE GAP.

What time was Epstein found?

OIG and archive context confirm discovery on August 10, 2019, but the current public manifest does not include a primary minute-level discovery timestamp — labeled SOURCE GAP (MCC-EVT-015).

Who was assigned to monitor him?

MCC staff were responsible for 30-minute counts and rounds per BOP policy; OIG found those rounds were not conducted and records were falsified (MCC-EVT-011, MCC-EVT-012). Named individual assignment sheets are not fully extracted on this page.

Why was he alone?

OIG states Epstein's cellmate was transferred and he was left without a replacement cellmate (MCC-EVT-009). Control-spine anchors raise alone-status awareness near the 10 p.m. count (MCC-EVT-010).

What happened to the surveillance cameras?

EFTA00039416 names CH0080 file windows for Aug. 9–10; OIG separately cites camera malfunctions (MCC-EVT-013). File lists and malfunctions are held as a contradiction — not merged into one conclusion.

Which records were falsified?

OIG found BOP count and round documentation was falsified for Epstein's unit (MCC-EVT-012).

What did the DOJ OIG conclude?

OIG cited failure to conduct counts/rounds, falsified records, camera issues, and leaving Epstein alone; OCME manner was suicide; OIG found no evidence disproving that determination (MCC-EVT-019).

What remains unresolved?

Exact discovery clock time, EMS/hospital chronology, full video continuity review, and complete command-chain documentation for cellmate replacement (MCC-EVT-015, MCC-EVT-017, MCC-EVT-008 vs MCC-EVT-013).
