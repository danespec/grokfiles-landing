How Not to Overread Flight Logs

A flight-log or tail-number record may show an aircraft, trip, route, passenger listing, registration, or source artifact. It does not by itself prove purpose, knowledge, conduct, relationship depth, or criminality.

Back to MethodologyOpen ArchiveLive changelog

What this can show

  • An aircraft tail number or registration appears in a record.
  • A trip, route, date, airport, passenger listing, or source artifact may be present.
  • A flight-log record may justify a follow-up source check.
  • A tail number can point readers toward FAA, flight-log, or archive source-chain review.

What this does not prove

  • Flight-log presence does not prove purpose.
  • A passenger listing does not prove knowledge, intent, agreement, or conduct.
  • A route does not prove coordination.
  • Tail-number ownership or movement does not prove relationship depth.
  • Chronology does not prove causation.
  • A missing or partial flight record is not a finding.

How Grok Archive Hub uses this

The archive treats aviation records as source-chain material. A useful aviation read needs a source path, date context, route context, registration context, passenger/source caveats, and a clear statement of what the record does not establish.
