New FBI Vault Parts 12–13: what the records show

FBI Vault added Parts 12 and 13 of its “Records regarding the review of the Investigative Holdings related to Jeffrey Epstein” series on October 8, 2026. The files provide contemporaneous 2025 review and transfer records. They are not newly created October 2026 investigation findings.

Primary files: FBI Vault Part 12 (19 PDF pages) · FBI Vault Part 13 (32 PDF pages). Page references below are PDF page numbers, not underlying Bates folio numbers. Respect redacted information and do not infer victim or witness identities.

Three documented leads

  1. Witness-list reconciliation, March 20, 2025: Part 12 PDF page 1 shows FBI internal correspondence asking colleagues to compare several versions of a victim/witness identification list and identify new entries. It does not establish a purported offender “client list,” and these lists must not be conflated. Original PDF.
  2. Transfers and redaction guidance, February–March 2025: Part 13 PDF pages 1–2 contain an “Epstein Overview” timeline describing records delivered to DOJ and additional review instructions dated March 22. The footnote characterizes one list as including victims and family members, not exclusively crime victims; its tally is not a criminal client count. Original PDF.
  3. July 3, 2025 coordination: Part 13 PDF page 9 contains emails between FBI and DOJ officials discussing edits to a public statement and video-hosting constraints. The correspondence substantiates coordination and a technical concern, not why any later release or investigative conclusion was adopted. Review the page in context.
October 25 update — Part 14 now public: The FBI's October 22 release adds a January 5, 2026 workflow email and January 4 EFTA review protocol. Read GAH’s independently sourced Part 14 report. Parts 12–13 below remain separate October 8 disclosures.

What remains unproven

This limited review does not establish intentional deletion, an illicit instruction to shield third parties, or a mismatch between any particular promised and published file count. The apparent shift in 2025 redaction scope requires comparison against applicable law, review protocols, actual released objects, and any contemporaneous notices. A redacted name or document gap is a question for further corroboration.
