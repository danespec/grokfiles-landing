Inside Gratitude America's Money Trail: $10M In, $8.45M Between Its Own Accounts, and a French Inquiry

An October 2015 bank-derived exhibit documents a $10 million credit. The same record follows years of account movements—but most of the 2016 outflows shown were transfers to accounts belonging to the charity itself, not grants. Later released records document a French financial-intelligence inquiry and an IRS accountant’s review. None of those documents, by themselves, establish a crime.

The line GAH will not cross

A bank-derived transaction schedule covers one money-market account, not the full financial history of a foundation. A transfer to another account held by the same legal entity is not a grant. A suspicious activity report is not a finding of financial crime. A file's investigative label is not an indictment or conviction. The full underlying ledgers, three SARs, French wire receipt and IRS forensic report remain outstanding.

1. The $10 million entry—and the money trail it actually traces

The nine-page released bank-derived document EFTA00080260, Exhibits A and E, identifies an October 14, 2015 transfer of $10,000,000 from BV70 LLC to Gratitude America Ltd. at reference DB-SDNY-0004999. Exhibit E describes the amount as the principal funding of the charity's Deutsche Bank money-market deposit account (MMDA). It is a bank-compiled schedule presented as an exhibit, not every original monthly statement. Dechert LLP's SEC-filed January 2021 review, section VI, separately reports a $10 million donation by Leon Black to Gratitude America in October 2015. That review is an outside-counsel report, not an SEC determination of tax treatment or legality.

The 2015 Form 990-PF financial summary reports $10,003,000 of contributions in that fiscal year. The $3,000 difference between the aggregate tax-return figure and the identified $10 million donor amount does not, by itself, identify another contributor or a missing deposit.

These amounts are outgoing postings from the tracked MMDA. They are not a reconstructed end balance, an accusation of disguised charitable spending, or proof that dollars from the October 2015 deposit can be traced individually through later recipient accounts.

2. The brokerage bridge: a $5 million wire and three named trades

On March 2, 2016, Kahn wrote that a $5,000,000 wire had just been sent. Financial adviser Andrew Atlas then circulated a short Gratitude America trading report (EFTA00643676) listing purchases of 800 LNKD shares at $119.1347, 7,500 BAC shares at $13.3725 and 3,200 EDIT shares at $31.8652. Those three stated quantities and prices total $297,570.15 in reported-order notional. They do not explain the remaining approximately $4.7 million of the claimed $5 million funding wire, nor are these emails complete independent settlement confirmations.

In a separate March 8 correspondence chain (EFTA00704168), a participant used the term unrealized for EDIT's market appreciation. That language describes a paper gain at the quoted moment, not a verified realized profit or cash withdrawal. The missing Morgan Stanley statements and confirmations remain the receipts required to reconstruct the actual brokerage balances, sales and later uses.

Why the tax returns do not equal the MMDA

The foundation's filed 2016 Form 990-PF, Part I and Schedule XV, shows $580,000 in grants paid. Of that amount, $325,000 appears as outgoing external-charity payments in Exhibit E's single MMDA schedule; the remaining $255,000 is simply not traced by this exhibit.

GAH's earlier review of the original 2018 return identifies $891,440 in reported grants. The MMDA exhibit shows only $53,493 in 2018 outgoing payments to outside recipients. The $837,947 difference is not traced by this one-account schedule. An IRS-derived ProPublica filing index corroborates the 2018 filing and its general financial picture; the original granular 2018 return could not be downloaded anew during this publication pass, so the $891,440 figure is explicitly attributed to the earlier GAH original-form review.

Do not call those differences missing grants. Payments could have originated from the foundation's checking, investment or FirstBank accounts omitted from Exhibit E. Original bank postings, disbursement instruments, and grantee credit records are the appropriate next source layer.

4. The French FIU inquiry and references to three SARs

A three-page released FBI/FinCEN correspondence chain, EFTA00153569–571, records a French financial-intelligence inquiry via the Egmont information-sharing network. A January 17, 2020 message says France's TRACFIN unit was examining the relationship between Gratitude America Ltd. and Association pour la Promotion de la Politique Culturelle, concerning a €50,000 transfer from the foundation's Deutsche Bank account. The message described concerns about an unclear purpose and possible illicit origin. Those are suspicions in an intelligence request, not proven criminal facts.

In March 6, 2020 correspondence, a FinCEN detailee said research located three suspicious activity reports (SARs) related to Gratitude America and asked the FBI about sharing an Egmont response and a spreadsheet with the French FIU. The public three-page exhibit does not supply the underlying SAR texts, the spreadsheet, French investigative findings or a final disposition. A SAR is a reporting mechanism for potentially suspicious activity and should never be treated as a judicial finding.

One apparent research lead must remain unjoined: GAH's earlier examination of the 2018 tax-return grant schedule identified a $57,897 grant to a similarly named association in Paris. The €50,000 FIU inquiry and the $57,897 reported grant are not established as one transaction. That would require a dated wire advice, bank debit and credit, beneficiary identifier, currency rate and tax-period reconciliation. A similar name and plausible currency conversion are insufficient.

5. An IRS contract accountant reviewed the accounts—what is missing?

A released one-page FBI import form dated April 26, 2021 (EFTA00128841) records an IRS contract forensic accountant's review of bank accounts for Gratitude America Ltd. The form was imported under FBI case ID 272B-SJ-3262541, bearing a case caption referencing Southern Trust, Southern Country International and wire fraud.

That verifies the existence of an investigative account-review step and its relationship to an FBI case file. It does not reveal the accountant's actual analysis, the enclosed materials, a recommendation, a charge, or the investigation's result. A case title containing wire fraud is not evidence that the named charity or any listed recipient was convicted or even charged.

7. What the documents leave open

  1. Complete account movement: Deutsche Bank monthly statements, internal checking ledgers, Morgan Stanley/Citibank brokerage statements and FirstBank Puerto Rico statements. The exhibit names these accounts without showing their full downstream disposition.
  2. The French wire: original €50,000 debit and beneficiary receipt; reconcile independently against any tax-return grant to the Paris association. No assumption of a match.
  3. FinCEN follow-up: only lawfully public Egmont correspondence or other authorized records. Do not seek to publish protected SAR contents merely because they are referenced.
  4. The IRS review: the actual accountant's enclosed report, if publicly released, and any subsequent official filing or judicial disposition.
  5. Investment exit and tax treatment: full trade confirmations, disposition records and final 990-PF grant-disbursement source records.
  6. Separate historical leads: Dechert's August 2015 unsubstantiated $5M acknowledgment letter, 2015 email date mismatches and a missing financial model are not additional confirmed bank receipts. They are addressed in GAH's related documentary audit and remain separate source questions.

The findings do not add up to an accusation

This investigation does not establish that the $10 million was illegal, that the account transfers were concealed grants, that the foundation or its named officers committed tax fraud or money laundering, that an outside recipient knowingly received improper funds, or that investigators reached a finding of wrongdoing. It establishes an auditable transaction sequence, a partial flow categorization, and recorded intelligence/accounting inquiries with material source gaps.
