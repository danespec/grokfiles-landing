A Proposed $200 Million Saudi–Israeli Healthcare Fund, Documented but Not Funded

Letters and emails from May–August 2015 document a detailed investment concept, proposed participants and a request for investor discretion. They stop short of establishing a licensed, subscribed or operating fund.

Grok Archive Hub Editorial Desk · October 12, 2026 · Documentary evidence audit

Editorial finding: Emails and draft documents from 2015 show a proposed Luxembourg life-sciences fund intended to attract Saudi investors and invest mainly in Israeli companies. The records do not establish that the fund was launched or funded.

The May 2015 proposal

A May 21 letter of intent from SAK Arabia describes a best-efforts commitment to recruit prospective investors toward a $200 million target. This is not evidence of funds received or committed. An accompanying presentation names Ehud Barak, Benad Goldwasser and Paz Tuval as a proposed investment team and describes a roughly 80% Israel / 20% Europe allocation.

The July draft

A lawyer-reviewed, expressly nonbinding letter discussed a 2% annual management fee, 20% carried interest and a seven-year initial term. Barak approved the circulating draft in a July 2 email. That reply does not prove an executed investment-management contract.

The August correspondence

On August 5, Goldwasser described anticipated talks in Paris involving representatives of prospective Saudi investors and Edmond de Rothschild personnel. He wanted investor identities to remain discreet and requested use of the group's name. Fabrice Aidan replied that he was willing to hear what the representatives proposed. The reviewed reply does not authorize a new fund.

What remains open

No Luxembourg incorporation, signed management agreement, capital subscriptions or completed investment were confirmed in this audit. Investor confidentiality can serve legitimate purposes; the correspondence alone does not establish unlawful conduct.
