Trump Was Running for President. The Moscow Project Was Still Alive.

Grok Archive Hub Editorial Desk · Published October 22, 2026

The Trump campaign-records series · Inspect the sources

The decisive Moscow fact is a date correction: the proposed project did not end in January 2016, as Michael Cohen initially told Congress. His later criminal case put the discussions into approximately June 2016. That moved the business pursuit well into Trump’s presidential campaign.

Read the criminal information, paragraphs 4 and 7 together with DOJ’s guilty-plea and sentencing entry. A charging document by itself is an allegation; here the official case record supplies the separate guilty-plea outcome.

There were two Moscow efforts, not one continuous deal

Mueller’s report separates the 2013–2014 Crocus Group proposal from the later 2015–2016 I.C. Expert / Cohen / Felix Sater effort. The earlier project stayed at the planning stage. The later effort produced a Trump-signed letter of intent transmitted in November 2015. The report also reproduces Sater’s November 3 attempt to frame the project as politically useful. Sater’s pitch is evidence of his proposal, not proof that Putin accepted it. Volume I, printed pp. 67–71.

Why the January-versus-June distinction matters

A project ending before the first votes and a project continuing during the primary contest create different disclosure questions. The discrepancy is therefore more than a rounding error in a businessman’s schedule. It changes which campaign statements and political milestones readers should compare with the negotiation record.

It does not follow that proposing a building was itself a crime, that a completed building existed, or that a prospective Russian business partner controlled the campaign. Those are separate propositions. The strongest version of this story stays with the documented overlap and the proven false statement.

Trump’s response belongs next to the accusation

On November 29, 2018, Trump told reporters that the project was an opportunity he chose not to pursue, said there would have been nothing wrong with doing it while campaigning, and accused Cohen of lying to reduce his punishment. He emphasized that the project was never completed. These are Trump’s stated defenses, not a reversal of Cohen’s plea. Read the opening exchanges in the transcript.

That response addresses completion and legality. Cohen’s case addresses the accuracy of his account to Congress. The two questions cannot be substituted for each other: a project can fail to reach construction and still have been pursued longer than a witness first admitted.

The broader Russia finding must stay visible

Mueller reported Russian election interference but did not establish that campaign members conspired or coordinated with the Russian government in its election-interference activities. That is the report’s stated investigative conclusion, not a finding that every contact was invented. Volume I, printed pp. 1–2.

This article concerns the Moscow business chronology. It does not use Cohen’s lie as a shortcut to an unproven election-conspiracy charge against Trump.

What would move this story beyond a recap?

  • The actual commercial instrument: compare the complete signed letter of intent with the report’s description; distinguish indicative terms from binding commitments.
  • The communication chain: identify dates, sender, recipient and confirmed replies, rather than treating an intermediary’s claimed access as actual Kremlin approval.
  • The disclosure comparison: attach the exact contemporaneous public statement before calling it a contradiction; completed investments and active negotiations are not identical categories.
  • The missing financial record: do not assert an undisclosed payment, completed transfer or payoff without the transaction evidence.

The durable headline is the overlap between campaign and business, supported by a corrected sworn-account record. The next original reporting task is to acquire and compare the underlying exhibits, not inflate the court outcome.

Source notes and how to verify

  1. Mueller report, Volume I

    Type: Official investigative report. Locate: Printed pp. 1–2, 67–71; page numbers are report pagination, not viewer count.

    Selected sections reviewed. Findings and declination language must stay together; underlying exhibits are distinct records.

  2. United States v. Cohen, 18-cr-850: criminal information

    Type: Filed charging document underlying guilty plea. Locate: Paragraphs 4 and 7, printed pp. 2–8.

    Read with the plea/sentencing record. Describes Cohen’s false statements; not a conviction of Trump.

  3. DOJ Special Counsel case archive: Cohen entry

    Type: Official case-outcome index. Locate: U.S. v. Michael Cohen, 1:18-cr-850.

    The Moscow false-statements sentence is distinct from Cohen’s other criminal case.

  4. Trump’s November 29, 2018 remarks to reporters

    Type: Presidential remarks transcript. Locate: Opening Cohen / private-business exchange.

    Trump’s response and characterization, not an independent adjudication.
