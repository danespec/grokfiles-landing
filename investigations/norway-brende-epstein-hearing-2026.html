Norway’s Epstein Hearing: The 2017 “Norwegian Friend” Email and Børge Brende’s 2018 Timeline

Børge Brende told Norway’s parliament that he had no contact with Jeffrey Epstein while serving as foreign minister and first met him in 2018. Lawmakers then tested that chronology against a September 2017 email in which Terje Rød-Larsen told Epstein that “my Norwegian friend and I” were coming to a meeting. The hearing did not establish that the unnamed friend was Brende. It did establish the contradiction question the committee put on the public record.

Core finding. The September 30 hearing places three records beside one another: Brende’s sworn/public testimony that he first met Epstein in 2018; a 2017 Rød-Larsen/Epstein email referring to an unnamed “Norwegian friend”; and a 2018 Brende/Epstein exchange about the World Economic Forum and the UN. The first two create an identity-and-chronology question. They do not, by themselves, identify the unnamed friend or prove a pre-2018 Brende-Epstein meeting.

What Brende told the committee

At the September 30, 2026 open hearing of the Storting’s Standing Committee on Scrutiny and Constitutional Affairs, Brende said he had no contact with Epstein while foreign minister and that the first time he met Epstein was in 2018, after leaving office. He described the later contact as three dinners plus email and SMS exchanges.

Brende told the committee that, knowing what he knows now, he would not have had contact with Epstein and that he should have investigated Epstein’s background. He said he took responsibility for that failure. He also connected the episode to his decision to leave the World Economic Forum.

The September 2017 “Norwegian friend” email

Committee member Hege Bae Nyholt raised a September 19, 2017 exchange between Terje Rød-Larsen and Epstein. According to the hearing transcript, Rød-Larsen wrote that “my Norwegian friend and I” would come to a 9 p.m. meeting, and Epstein confirmed.

Nyholt asked Brende directly whether he was the unnamed friend. Brende did not identify himself as that person. He reiterated that he first met Epstein in spring 2018 and said he did not meet Epstein in September 2017. He added that it would be strange to describe him as a friend if they had never met.

Evidence boundary. The phrase “my Norwegian friend” is an identity lead, not an identification. Without a source that names the person, a contemporaneous calendar, travel record, attendee list, message from Brende, or another receipt tying him to the meeting, GAH leaves the identity unresolved.

The September 2018 WEF/UN exchange

The committee also confronted Brende with a September 16, 2018 email exchange after he had left government. Epstein suggested that the World Economic Forum could replace the United Nations. The hearing transcript records Brende replying: “Exactly - we need a new global architecture. World Economic Forum (Davos) is uniquely positioned - public private.”

Brende told the committee that the private exchange was unserious and that he never believed WEF could replace the UN. He said he should have rejected the idea rather than replying politely and quickly.

The timing matters. The exchange is from 2018, after Brende’s term as foreign minister ended in October 2017. The email therefore establishes a later private exchange; it does not, without additional records, establish that Brende advanced that idea while in government.

Resignation, not an evidenced “ouster”

The World Economic Forum’s February 26, 2026 public statement says Brende decided to step down as President and CEO. The board said it respected his decision and that an independent outside-counsel review found no additional concerns beyond what had previously been disclosed. WEF appointed Alois Zwinggi to lead the organization.

That official wording matters. GAH does not characterize the departure as an “ouster” because the public WEF record cited here describes a resignation. Brende told parliament that he resigned because of the Epstein-contact issue; the WEF statement said he believed the Forum should continue without distractions.

What the committee was actually investigating

The hearing was broader than Brende’s personal contacts. The committee opened its control case after U.S. Department of Justice disclosures concerning Epstein and framed questions around foreign-service culture, controls, possible favoritism or influence, aid administration, and the use of networks and development funds in campaigns for positions in international organizations.

That scope is important because it separates two different questions: what Brende personally did after leaving government, and whether Norway’s institutions had adequate controls during the relevant years. The September 2018 email belongs to the first category unless a source chain connects it backward to official decisions.

What this record does not establish

  • It does not establish that Børge Brende was the unnamed “Norwegian friend” in the September 2017 email.
  • It does not establish that Brende met or communicated with Epstein while serving as Norway’s foreign minister.
  • It does not establish that the 2018 WEF/UN email reflected Norwegian government policy.
  • It does not establish that WEF’s board forced Brende out; the cited public statement describes his decision to step down.
  • It does not establish corruption, improper influence, or misuse of public funds by Brende.

Why this is a live open question

The strongest unresolved issue is narrow: can the unnamed “Norwegian friend” in the September 2017 exchange be identified from a primary source? If the answer is no, the correct public record remains exactly where the hearing left it—an unresolved identity lead beside Brende’s 2018-first-meeting testimony.

That is also where GAH’s method matters. A chronology tension is worth investigating. It is not permission to fill the missing identity with a guess.
