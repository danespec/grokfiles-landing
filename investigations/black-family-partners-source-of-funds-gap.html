Where Did the $5.5 Million Come From? The Black Family Partners Source-of-Funds Gap

The receiving-side bank record gets us farther than it first appears. Financial Trust’s November 30 JPMorgan statement names Black Family Partners as originator, Bank of America as the sending bank, and originator account 000001200135. Separate October records show that same Black Family Partners account moving tens of millions of dollars. But the public record reviewed here still does not identify the immediate credit or balance component that funded the November 30 payment.

Grok Archive Hub Editorial Desk · October 8, 2026

Open the buyer-side chainOpen EFTA01510783

Core finding. The November 30 payment can be tied to Black Family Partners’ Bank of America account ending 0135. That same account is independently visible in October 3 wire records. What is still missing is the buyer-side November statement or matching debit advice showing the account balance immediately before the $5,500,030 transfer and the specific inflows supporting it.

Source provenance

Three records define the problem.

EFTA01510783 · Nov. 30 receiving-side JPMorgan recordShows a $5,500,030 CHIPS credit to Financial Trust. The description names Black Family Partners, Bank of America, and originator account 000001200135. EFTA00589888–EFTA00589921 · Oct. 3 Black-family transfer packetShows account 000001200135 as a Black Family Partners Bank of America account moving $19 million and $33 million on October 3, with a related internal-transfer summary. EFTA00634299 · Nov. 26 sale-package emailStates the aggregate purchase price was $5.5 million and that closing was targeted for November 30.

1. The November 30 wire identifies the buyer-side account

EFTA01510783 records a $5,500,030 CHIPS credit into Financial Trust Company’s JPMorgan account. The wire description identifies Black Family Partners, L.P. as the ordering party and Bank of America as the sending bank.

More importantly for source-of-funds work, the payment detail contains ORGE/000001200135. That account number matters because it appears independently in a Black Family Partners Bank of America wire advice from October 3.

This lets the archive make a narrower and stronger statement than before: the November 30 receipt does not merely name Black Family Partners generically. It points to the same BofA account that survives in buyer-side records.

2. October 3 proves account 0135 was moving large sums

The October transfer packet at EFTA00589888 and the related EFTA00589905 representation contains a Bank of America wire advice for Black Family Partners account 000001200135. On October 3, that account shows two outgoing wires:

  • $19 million to LBF Holdings LLC; and
  • $33 million to the Black Family 1997 Trust.

The same packet’s internal transfer summary says $20 million had moved from LBF Holdings account 5824 into Black Family Partners account 0135 as repayment of a promissory note, along with a separate $164,318 interest payment from an LBF account.

Those records establish that account 0135 was part of a large, active family-office cash-management network roughly eight weeks before the Financial Trust purchase.

3. What the October records do not prove

Critical boundary. October 3 account activity is not evidence that any particular October inflow remained in account 0135 until November 30. Money is fungible, and the public record reviewed here does not supply a continuous account ledger from October 3 through the closing date.

The October packet therefore helps identify the account and its financial context. It does not close the source-of-funds question for the November 30 purchase.

4. The tempting “source of funds” page is for a different transaction

The first page of EFTA00589888 is literally labeled “Source of funds.” It lists sources including a September distribution from Apollo Credit Opportunity Fund, dividends on Apollo Investment Corporation shares, recent art-sale proceeds, liquidation of an equity account managed by Neuberger & Berman, cash held in trusts, and other internal family-office funds.

But the packet itself identifies those transfers as being made “in advance of Phaidon investment.” The destination structure runs through Black family trusts and PLB LLC. That page cannot be silently repurposed as the source-of-funds schedule for the Financial Trust purchase a month later.

Research rule: a source-of-funds schedule from one transaction is not a source-of-funds schedule for another transaction merely because the same family office, accounts, or personnel appear.

6. The November receiving-side statement adds context, not buyer funding

Financial Trust’s full November JPMorgan statement, EFTA01556835, shows its own account rising from a beginning net market value of roughly $4.247 million to an ending value of roughly $9.924 million. The $5,500,030 Black Family Partners credit is the obvious major cash event in that change.

That tells us what happened on the seller side. It does not tell us what funded the buyer side.

7. Why account 0135 matters anyway

The account linkage materially narrows the missing receipt. We are no longer searching for an unknown Black-family account somewhere in the banking system. The source chain now points to a specific Bank of America account: 000001200135.

The highest-value missing object is therefore a November 2012 statement, transaction history, or debit advice for that account covering the period immediately before and including November 30.

8. What this investigation does not claim

  • It does not claim the November 30 payment came from Apollo Credit Opportunity Fund.
  • It does not claim the November 30 payment came from art-sale proceeds, a Neuberger & Berman liquidation, or any trust listed in the Phaidon source-of-funds schedule.
  • It does not claim the October 3 $20 million loan repayment remained in account 0135 through November 30.
  • It does not characterize the Financial Trust transaction as money laundering, tax fraud, concealment, or a sham sale.
  • It does not treat the absence of the buyer-side debit advice as evidence that the payment was improper.

9. The exact missing receipts

  • Bank of America Nov. 30 debit advice for account 000001200135 matching the $5,500,030 CHIPS credit.
  • November 2012 Bank of America statement for Black Family Partners account 000001200135.
  • Intervening account activity between October 3 and November 30 sufficient to identify the balance immediately before closing.
  • Closing-side tax/accounting entry on the Black Family Partners books identifying how the purchase was funded and booked.

10. The source-of-funds question is now narrow enough to test

The current record does not justify a dramatic conclusion. It does something more useful: it reduces the unknown to a specific account, a specific bank, a specific date, and a small set of missing records.

Black Family Partners account 000001200135 at Bank of America is the buyer-side account to chase. Until its November ledger is recovered, the immediate source of the $5.5 million remains open.
