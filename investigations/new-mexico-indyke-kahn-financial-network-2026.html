New Mexico Sues Epstein's Attorney and Accountant: Inside the Estate, Zorro Ranch and Money Trail

New Mexico Attorney General Raúl Torrez filed a 49-page federal civil lawsuit against Darren K. Indyke and Richard D. Kahn, individually and as co-executors of Jeffrey Epstein's estate and administrators of his 1953 Trust. The filing lays out alleged payment, corporate-control, property and travel mechanisms. This analysis separates what the State alleges from what the filing itself proves and which underlying bank and flight records still need an independent join.

U.S. District Court, District of New Mexico · State of New Mexico ex rel. Torrez v. Indyke et al. · No. 1:26-cv-03543 · ECF 1 · 49 pages · Filed October 21, 2026.

Legal status: This is a civil complaint, not a finding of liability or a criminal conviction. Allegations described below are attributed to the State. Indyke and Kahn, through counsel, deny knowingly assisting Epstein's crimes. Their lawyer said neither witnessed abuse and disputed the State's failure to interview them before suing. No guilt is inferred from a name, filing, payment, entity position or flight record.

Three things that changed today

  1. State action: The New Mexico Attorney General sued Indyke and Kahn, the Epstein estate and the 1953 Trust under federal trafficking and state racketeering civil theories. Complaint p. 1
  2. Estate-control dispute: The State seeks to stop estate distributions while the case proceeds and seeks survivor damages, restitution, disgorgement and other relief. Potential trust bequests are $50 million and $25 million, contingent on remaining funds; the complaint says the estate exceeded $107 million as of June 30, 2026. Complaint p. 7
  3. Record joins: The complaint connects claimed control of Zorro Ranch entities, accounts and cash distributions with private and commercial travel. It does not itself supply a verified passenger-by-passenger roster for every New Mexico flight. Complaint p. 10 · Complaint p. 13

Flight logs, ranch travel and what can actually be proven

The State alleges that flight records show travel to Zorro Ranch during 1997–2018 (§51, p.13), and that travel coordinators used private and commercial flights (§38, p.10). It specifically recounts a survivor's account of a commercial ticket from Phoenix to Santa Fe (§76, p.17), and alleges accounts overseen by Kahn bought airline tickets (§154, p.34). The complaint does not attach a complete, independently reconciled passenger manifest in those sections. Complaint p. 10 · Complaint p. 13 · Complaint p. 17 · Complaint p. 34

GAH evidence rule: A tail number identifies an aircraft, not every passenger. A name in a log does not prove knowledge of abuse. An airport arrival does not establish the location or purpose of anyone's activity after landing. This is why the flight-log interpretation standard is essential here.

What New Mexico actually asks the court to do

The complaint's prayer for relief requests declarations under racketeering theories; divestment and restrictions on certain interests; compensatory and punitive damages, restitution and disgorgement; and an order preventing the estate's co-executors from distributing estate assets during this litigation. Some remedies are requested for survivors under 18 U.S.C. §1595 and New Mexico law; none is granted merely by filing. Complaint p. 47 · Complaint p. 48

The complaint names the 1953 Trust as an asset holder, expressly not because the State says the trust itself committed racketeering. Complaint p. 6

Indyke and Kahn's denial

Daniel Weiner, representing both men, rejected the allegation that they knowingly facilitated Epstein's abuse. In his statement to Reuters, he said no woman accused them of personally committing or witnessing sexual abuse and criticized the State's decision to file without interviewing them. This is the defense position; it is not a ruling on disputed facts. Read Reuters' October 21 report.

Survivors Julia Molchanova and Anouska De Georgiou supported the civil action at New Mexico DOJ's announcement, seeking accountability beyond Epstein and Maxwell. Read the State's announcement and survivors' published statement.

The next five records GAH needs to close this investigation

  1. Zorro Ranch ownership: the December 19, 2011 deed, Cypress annual reports, corporate officers and any title-transfer consideration.
  2. Accounts and 97 withdrawals: underlying JPMorgan/Deutsche Bank transaction rows, currency reports or documentation, dated signer authorization and competing explanations.
  3. HBRK / payroll: original HBRK and professional-fee invoices, recipient-account statements, charitable foundation books and probate loan records.
  4. Actual travel records: original Maxwell exhibits, leg-by-leg pilot logs, private FBO records and commercial ticket/receipt data; join only verifiable dates and airport codes.
  5. Post-filing docket: summons, service, answers, preliminary-injunction motions, responsive exhibits and court orders in 1:26-cv-03543.

GAH has not claimed to possess or independently authenticate these complete underlying sets. Exact passenger-account-name joins remain unresolved. Email corrections and source records through our corrections policy.
