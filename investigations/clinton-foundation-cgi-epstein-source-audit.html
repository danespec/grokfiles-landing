Clinton Foundation, CGI and Epstein: What the Financial and Organizational Records Establish

A source-bound audit of the proposed 2004 Publicis $1 million transfer, a 2006 C.O.U.Q. Foundation grant, 2013 TerraMar CGI participation, testimony about CGI origins, and the Foundation financial record. Key payment and causal claims remain unverified.

Grok Archive Hub Editorial Desk · October 16, 2026 · Research in progress; sources and open questions maintained separately

Examine the receiptsUnresolved questionsResearch source ledger

Current finding — October 16, 2026. Public documents establish an anticipated $1 million transfer in 2004 Publicis/Clinton-project correspondence, a reported $25,000 charitable grant in a 2006 C.O.U.Q. return, and later CGI connections described in testimony and organizational records. They do not establish that the 2004 transfer settled, who funded it, that CGI commitments were Foundation receipts, or that Epstein-related activity caused the Foundation’s post-2014 revenue decline. No wrongdoing by Bill Clinton, Hillary Clinton, Doug Band, or the Clinton Foundation is established by these sources.

Evidence types are not interchangeable

An email instructing someone to send money is not a bank posting. A grant in a donor-side tax return is not the same as a recipient bank statement. A CGI Commitment to Action can represent a third party’s promise and cannot be assumed to pass through the Clinton Foundation’s accounts. Testimony establishes what witnesses said, not whether every recollection is correct.

1. The proposed $1 million transfer: a contemporaneous lead, not a completed payment

A message dated October 7, 2004, attributed to Publicis Events' Mary Morrison and appearing in the released EFTA correspondence, supplies instructions for an expected $1 million wire and asks to be told when the transfer has been made. The quoted project reference identifies a Publicis Events Clinton-related project. See EFTA00579174 and the follow-up chain, EFTA00579182.

The messages describe anticipation and requested coordination. They do not constitute a receiving-bank credit, prove Epstein personally paid the money, identify what performance the project delivered, or show that any proceeds reached the Clinton Foundation. Bank account, routing and individual contact numbers are deliberately not reproduced here.

2. The $25,000 C.O.U.Q. entry is a separate charitable record

A C.O.U.Q. Foundation Form 990-PF grant schedule in the released EFTA00224262 return compilation lists the William J. Clinton Foundation as a recipient of a $25,000 unrestricted grant dated July 18, 2006. The line is donor-side tax-report evidence of the reported grant, not a verified record of its clearing at the recipient’s bank. This entry must not be added to or confused with the prospective 2004 Publicis transfer.

The existence of a reported donation neither establishes knowledge of Epstein’s criminal acts by a grant recipient nor proves exchange for favors. Matching donor-side disbursement records and recipient-side acknowledgement is an outstanding verification step.

3. The different accounts of who helped create CGI

In a July 2007 defense submission, EFTA00176507, attorneys advocating for Jeffrey Epstein described him as part of a group that conceived CGI. That is a litigant's characterization advanced for its client's benefit, not a finding of fact.

In Maxwell’s July 25, 2025 DOJ interview transcript, Maxwell recounted her own involvement in startup-related work while distinguishing that role from originating the concept. In the House-released June 30, 2026 Doug Band interview, Band described his role and expressed disagreement or lack of recollection about the broader founding claims. These accounts should not be flattened into a single established origin story.

What would settle the issue is a contemporaneous documentary chain: dated concept proposals, planning meeting records, staff responsibilities, contracts with event producers and any actual payment records. GAH’s existing 36-claim Band transcript audit remains the associated witness-level source analysis.

4. Maxwell’s TerraMar project and CGI participation

Maxwell's TerraMar Project was associated with a 2013 CGI oceans-related commitment and public recognition, according to contemporary descriptions and later testimony. TerraMar was a distinct nonprofit with its own IRS tax filings. This public record supports examining the relationship but does not demonstrate that CGI or the Clinton Foundation transferred money to TerraMar, approved unlawful conduct, or knew of wrongdoing.

Our next check is the original dated CGI commitment entry and its approval, any reported delivery milestones, and a reconciliation of TerraMar’s independent filings. The terms commitment, recognition, donation, grant and completed payment refer to materially different events.

5. CGI’s annual-meeting pause and the Foundation’s financial filings

The Foundation’s own records describe CGI’s 2016 meeting as the final New York annual gathering of that series; the annual meetings returned in 2022. The Foundation also describes targeted work between those years, including a Puerto Rico gathering in 2019. See the Foundation-hosted September 2016 coverage and Foundation’s 2022 explanation.

The Foundation continued operating. Its publicly available Form 990 revenue series, for operating nonprofit EIN 31-1580204, shows a substantial change over this period:

That is an accounting observation, not evidence that Epstein, Maxwell, or any particular allegation caused the change. A causal finding requires a donor-by-donor and program-by-program reconciliation.

New 2016 receipt: reporters were already asking for founding records

A June 30, 2016 email, preserved as HOUSE_OVERSIGHT_029501, forwards questions by reporter Malia Zimmerman to attorney Alan Dershowitz about the 2007 defense letter claiming Epstein was among CGI's original conceivers. She asks whether formal founding or board documents support that description and what “original group” meant. Read the two-page original House Oversight record (029501).

Significance: This is a dated request for substantiation almost a decade before the 2025 Maxwell and 2026 Band interviews. The forwarded question does not establish that such records existed, or provide counsel's full response. CGI founding and staffing records remain the relevant test.

What GAH still needs before stronger claims can be made

  1. Publicis transfer: a dated originating debit and beneficiary credit matched to the specific invoice or project, with bank identifiers protected.
  2. C.O.U.Q. grant: supporting disbursement/check and Foundation-side acknowledgement for the reported July 2006 $25,000 contribution.
  3. CGI formation: original 2004–05 contracts, planning notes, staff roles and founding minutes to test the advocacy and witness accounts.
  4. TerraMar: archived CGI commitment and approval records, project outcomes, and tax-return funding reconciliation.
  5. Organization and revenue: original CGI-affiliate filings and year-by-year donor or program records distinguishing the annual meeting from the Foundation as a whole.

The working evidence tracker is GAH Issue #100; the dated October 16 source ledger is also public. Each unresolved claim stays labeled unresolved until a traceable original receipt can change its status.

Verification and editorial safeguards

Primary documents and official organizational records take precedence over interviews, legal advocacy, third-party document indices and unverified social claims. Some DOJ-hosted EFTA PDFs may require age verification. Reproducing a derived index is not a substitute for authenticating the original file. No victim-identifying details, financial account numbers or unredacted sensitive contacts are republished in this audit.

Finding boundary: Named associations, donations, planned transfers, CGI event attendance or testimonial references do not by themselves establish criminal knowledge, wrongdoing, a financial quid pro quo or institutional complicity. The story remains an ongoing source audit, not an adjudicated legal conclusion.
