Epstein’s 2013 Putin “Private Time” Suggestion and the 2018–2019 Qatar/Carbyne Chain

A corrected source audit strengthens two parts of the record at once: a May 2013 DOJ-released email contains Epstein’s explicit suggestion that Barak ask whether Vladimir Putin wanted “private time,” while the Qatar chain proceeds from Epstein’s introduction to a London meeting, a Carbyne pitch, a request to begin discussions, and confirmed direct contact with a Qatari systems integrator. The promised anti-drone follow-up remains missing.

Grok Archive Hub Editorial Desk · October 6, 2026 · Updated October 6, 2026

Core finding. The 2013 “private time” line is not leak-only: DOJ-released EFTA01975333 records Epstein telling Barak to let Putin know he would be in Moscow and to see whether Putin wanted private time, immediately after Barak asked Epstein not to share information with “our friends.” That establishes Epstein’s suggestion, not delivery to Putin or a meeting. Separately, the 2018–2019 Qatar record now joins end-to-end through an operational business-development contact: Epstein introduced Barak to Jabor Al Thani; a London meeting followed; Barak pitched Carbyne; Jabor’s security company asked to start discussions; Barak designated Carbyne CEO Amir Elichai; and Jabor connected Elichai to Lysys WLL, after which Elichai wrote that the contact had already reached him. The parallel anti-drone request is unresolved because the promised second email has not been located.

2013: the “private time” line is a primary DOJ record

On May 9, 2013, EFTA01975333 records Barak asking Epstein not to share information with “our friends.” Epstein replies that he will not, then suggests that Barak let Putin know he will be in Moscow and see whether Putin wants “private time.” The important correction is evidentiary: this wording appears in the public DOJ corpus, not merely in a leaked-mailbox reconstruction.

The boundary is equally important. The email does not show that Putin received a message, answered Barak, scheduled time, met Epstein, or communicated directly with Epstein. GAH therefore treats it as proof of Epstein’s suggestion about seeking private time with Putin, not proof that the proposed contact occurred. Public corpus search.

2015: an inquiry and a Putin reference, but no joined contact

EFTA02396786 preserves a June 18, 2015 Barak–Epstein thread in which Barak asks, “How about the Sultan?” Epstein says he had “already asked” and separately states that he understands Putin will give the next day’s keynote. The record does not identify whom Epstein asked, establish that the inquiry concerned Putin, or identify a Kremlin intermediary.

2017: a house visit, not an overnight stay

EFTA00450694 schedules Barak to visit Epstein’s New York house on May 9, 2017, with a roughly 5:00–6:20 p.m. window before theater tickets. It proves a scheduled house visit. It does not establish lodging or an overnight stay.

2018: Epstein introduces Barak and Jabor Al Thani

EFTA02609150 and EFTA02612143 preserve Epstein’s November introduction of Barak and Jabor Al Thani, reciprocal acknowledgment of that introduction, and arrangements for a December 19 meeting at 100 Knightsbridge in London. Post-meeting messages on December 21 confirm the meeting occurred. Jabor then asks Barak for more specific details about the security company they had discussed.

January 1, 2019: the Carbyne pitch

EFTA02607775 records Barak sending Jabor the “Qatar Presentation <> Carbyne 911” material with Epstein copied. Barak describes himself as Carbyne’s chairman and a leading investor, states an interest in participating in security for the 2022 Games, and proposes working through a European company selected by Jabor to “minimize Israeli profile.” The email proves a proposal was sent; it does not prove acceptance, payment, procurement or illegality.

January 20–February 4: the pitch becomes an active business-development sequence

The later thread materially strengthens the chain. On January 20, Jabor writes that his security-services company would like to commence a discussion regarding the Carbyne presentation and asks for a key contact. In the same message he separately asks whether Barak can assist with “any anti-drone services or technologies.”

On February 3, in EFTA02628004 and the fuller duplicate chain EFTA02628648, Barak says Carbyne would be glad to explore cooperation for the 2022 Games and names CEO Amir Elichai as the contact. Elichai replies that he is happy to follow up and asks for next steps. On February 4, Jabor identifies Dimitrios Demestichas, Country Manager of Lysys WLL, as his side’s contact. In EFTA02628761, Elichai replies minutes later that Demestichas had already contacted him. That establishes direct operational contact between Carbyne’s CEO and the Qatari-side integrator. Public document transcript.

The missing second email: what the record actually lets us say

The February 3 subject line is unusually explicit: “First of two emails.” In that email Barak says he will address the anti-drone issue separately the next day because he is “still waiting for some information.” GAH did not stop at the public index: we reconciled the duplicate EFTA chains, the local Barak/DOJ forensic indexes, and the original raw Barak mailbox archive stored on the project NAS. No matching second message or source naming the anti-drone supplier was located.

That means the strongest supportable conclusion is narrower than a vendor identification: Barak was waiting for information needed to answer Jabor’s separate anti-drone request, but the reviewed record does not identify the person, company, product or technology supplying that information. Absence from the reviewed corpus is not proof that the email never existed: it may not have copied Epstein, may sit outside the released DOJ set, or may survive in a later Barak mailbox export that is not in the located archive.

NAS-wide raw-mail audit: 40,976 messages checked

The original HTML mailbox archive on the GAH NAS contains 40,976 raw messages across two Barak accounts: 38,886 in ehbarak1@gmail.com and 2,090 in ehud.barak@hyperion-eb.com. GAH parsed date headers for 40,912 of them. The Gmail archive runs from April 2009 through a latest parsed message on March 12, 2018; the Hyperion account runs from March 2013 through March 23, 2016. That coverage boundary matters: the located raw archive ends before the January–February 2019 Qatar exchange and therefore cannot itself contain Barak’s promised 2019 second email.

Exact searches across the raw HTML produced no hit for anti-drone, counter-drone, Carbyne, Lysys, Demestichas, Qatar Presentation, First of two emails or Second of two emails. A second semantic pass found 204 messages containing drone/UAV/UAS language and flagged 67 broad counter-UAS-context candidates. Those candidates reduced to news and newsletters, military or policy material, consumer-drone mail, and one real Barak investment lane described below. None identified the 2019 anti-drone supplier. Machine-readable NAS audit.

Historical drone and radar leads tested — none identifies the 2019 supplier

The raw Gmail search did uncover a substantive drone lead. In October–November 2014 Barak sent Epstein material on Light & Strong, a proposed solar high-altitude persistent unmanned-aircraft project. The raw thread discusses technical diligence, Barak potentially serving as chairman and an investment of up to $1 million. The attached pitch describes a long-endurance solar UAV platform; it is not a counter-drone system. DOJ-released EFTA02593250 independently preserves Barak asking Epstein for his lawyer’s contact “re the Drone.” This establishes a genuine Barak–Epstein drone-investment lane, but no located document bridges Light & Strong to Jabor’s 2019 anti-drone request.

A second candidate was more technically relevant and therefore required separate review: Levitection / CWEW-Q. In April 2016 Barak sent the project to Epstein, described its team as Rafael veterans with advanced-radar experience, and proposed a Reporty-style investment structure. The attached investor brief identifies “Q” as a 70GHz electromagnetic/radar system intended to detect concealed guns, knives, rifles and explosive belts in public spaces. A July 2016 proposed term sheet, EFTA00599472, names an entity under Barak’s control as the proposed investor. Radar can be part of a counter-UAS stack, but the located Levitection material is a near-field concealed-weapons detection system, not an anti-drone product. No email, term sheet, patent record or later public project record located in this audit connects Levitection to Jabor’s 2019 request.

GAH also tested another plausible-looking branch instead of assuming it: EFTA02656769, dated May 21, 2018, records Epstein telling Peter Thiel, “FYI trae is meeting carbyne Ehud Barak co.” That Carbyne contact was searched against the later anti-drone hypothesis. No document-level join to Barak’s February 2019 anti-drone promise was found, so it is excluded from the affirmative supplier chain.

The February 2019 neighborhood closes without a second message

Across the local Barak/DOJ forensic indexes, the February 2019 records located with Jabor in this lane reduce to EFTA02627830, EFTA02628004, EFTA02628648 and EFTA02628761. Those are the “First of two emails” chain and its Carbyne/Lysys continuation. A numerically adjacent candidate, EFTA02629121, was opened and eliminated as unrelated. The indexed sequence then moves into unrelated Barak/Epstein material. That does not prove destruction or suppression; it defines the documentary break we can actually show.

Where the procurement trail points instead

There is also meaningful counter-evidence against treating this chain as a completed Carbyne/Qatar procurement. Barak attorney Bruce Goodman later told the Miami Herald that Barak conducted preliminary due diligence, that Carbyne was determined not to be suited to the World Cup role, and that Carbyne did not pursue a bid. That is an attributed participant-side account, not an independent procurement record. Miami Herald report.

Public Qatar reporting later documents other counter-UAS channels. In March 2021, Qatar’s Ministry of Defence Executive Committee announced Barzan Holdings as a partner for implementing an anti-drone system for the World Cup, while Rheinmetall Barzan Advanced Technologies described a Qatari counter-drone solution undergoing demonstrations and deployments. Separately, Smart Communication Systems says Qatar’s Ministry of Interior and the Supreme Committee selected SCS for a World Cup anti-drone deployment valued by SCS at $35 million, using U.S.-origin technology for which SCS held regional representation rights. These sources do not prove that one system constituted the entirety of Qatar’s counter-UAS architecture, but they provide no public procurement join back to Carbyne. What this investigation does not claim

  • It does not claim Epstein met, called or communicated directly with Vladimir Putin.
  • It does not claim the May 2013 suggestion resulted in any Putin meeting.
  • It does not infer the identity of the missing anti-drone supplier from Barak’s other investments or contacts; Light & Strong, Levitection, and the May 2018 “trae”/Carbyne lead were tested and did not produce a document-level bridge.
  • It does not claim Epstein represented an intelligence service or that the Qatar sequence had an intelligence purpose.
  • It does not claim Qatar awarded Carbyne a contract, paid Carbyne, or deployed Carbyne technology.
  • It does not treat the later Barzan/RBAT or SCS counter-UAS records as proof of the entirety of Qatar’s World Cup security architecture.

Why this moves the record forward

The update changes the evidentiary shape of the story. The 2013 Putin/private-time language is a primary public record. The 2019 Qatar sequence no longer stops at a pitch: it reaches a named Carbyne executive, a named Qatari-side integrator and confirmed direct contact. The anti-drone branch, however, now has a corpus-audited documentary break: the original NAS mail archive, the local DOJ/Barak indexes and the public EFTA chain were all tested, and plausible historical drone/Carbyne leads did not join to the promised second email. The missing “second of two emails” is therefore a defined open receipt slot—not permission to substitute a plausible vendor for an identified one.
