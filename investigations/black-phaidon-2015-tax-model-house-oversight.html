Epstein’s 2015 Phaidon Tax Model: The $38.7M–$44M Projection and Its Correction

House Oversight correspondence contains an October 2015 estimated capital-loss scenario; a second page corrects the assumed rate from 28% to 20%. A 2021 SEC-filed private investigation establishes broader advisory activity, not realization of this particular modeled tax benefit.

Grok Archive Hub Editorial Desk · October 16, 2026 · Verified original records; investigation ongoing

Read source evidenceAll investigations

Verified finding. An October 26, 2015 message proposes a hypothetical Phaidon/art-space loss of about $90–98 million and an initial $38.7–44 million tax-savings estimate. A later dated message explicitly revises one assumed component from 28% to 20%, making the combined modeled rate 35.8%. Neither message proves an executed sale, a claimed deduction, an IRS approval or a realized tax saving.

The initial model: what the released page actually states

The first page, House Oversight HOUSE_OVERSIGHT_030368, is a 2018 self-forward containing a message dated October 26, 2015, at 7:08 p.m. The writer describes an approximately $96 million combined basis in Phaidon and an art-space position, adds a hypothetical $10 million restructuring amount, assumes $8–15 million of sale proceeds, and projects an approximate $90–98 million long-term loss. Using a stated composite rate around 43%, the message estimates $38.7–44 million in savings. These are the sender's assumptions, not independently established tax basis or proceeds. Open the one-page House Oversight source scan (030368).

The source contains internal numerical approximations. We preserve its quoted ranges rather than retrospectively certifying the arithmetic or the legality of the proposed characterization.

The correction: later that evening the assumed rate changed

The released HOUSE_OVERSIGHT_023208 image begins with an October 26, 2015, 8:37 p.m. instruction to family-office advisers Brad Wechsler and Melanie Spinella. It says the gain-rate assumption should be 20%, not 28%, and gives a revised overall assumed rate of 35.8%. It also separately sketches hypothetical savings if both a boat and an airplane were sold. The page includes an October 27 operational task list covering trusts, art, aircraft, tax and bank-account administration. Inspect the original page image (023208) · Read the publicly indexed document

These are not two independent transactions. A revised modeling assumption means the earlier projected savings range must not be presented as final. No final revised transaction-specific dollar calculation or completed sale is proven by these pages.

What a later investigation independently confirms—and what it does not

The January 22, 2021 memorandum prepared by Dechert LLP for Apollo's Conflicts Committee, filed publicly as SEC Exhibit 99.1, reports that Leon Black paid Epstein $158 million during 2013–2017 for estate, tax, art, aircraft and family-office services. It describes Epstein giving tax-related advice on Phaidon, but does not authenticate realization of the particular $90–98 million loss or the October 2015 savings projections. Read the full SEC-filed Dechert memorandum.

Dechert's private investigation reports that its review found no evidence that Black, Apollo or family-office personnel were involved in Epstein's criminal activities. It also describes disagreements about the value and attribution of some of Epstein's work and disputes concerning compensation. This report is a counsel-prepared review submitted in a public securities filing, not an SEC adjudication of tax compliance.

Do not merge this plan with the other Black/Epstein money trails

The 2015 Phaidon/art-space loss scenario differs from the 2015–16 so-called step-up basis transaction discussed in Dechert's review, and from GAH's separate 2012 Financial Trust / Black Family Partners transaction audit. Dates, entity ownership, economic purpose, tax mechanism and proof of completion have to match before two document sets may be joined.

Likewise, Dechert's reported $158 million advisory payments cannot be used to infer that the contemplated Phaidon tax benefits were realized, or that such planning constituted misconduct.

Outstanding primary receipts

  1. Executed Phaidon/art-space sale and restructuring contracts, if any, with dated closing statements and identity of each economic owner.
  2. Independently documented tax basis, professional accounting memo, filed tax treatment and any IRS examination result.
  3. Complete dated message thread and independent recipient responses for the October 26–28, 2015 assumptions.
  4. Separate transaction mapping for aircraft, boat, 2015–16 step-up basis restructuring and older ESWW/Black Family Partners deals.

Current status: two original House document images and a public SEC-filed private review support the existence and revision of the proposed model. Its financial execution and ultimate legality remain unverified. Persons named in the correspondence are not accused of a crime by this analysis.
