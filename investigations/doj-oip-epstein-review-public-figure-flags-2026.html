DOJ Epstein Review Emails: Public-Figure Flags and Missing Rules

The Justice Department's October 9 release supplies contemporaneous 2025 correspondence about how FBI and DOJ reviewers handled the Epstein investigative files. It confirms instructions to flag additional prominent names for review and documents safeguards for victims. It does not establish that those names were automatically removed, that improper suppression occurred, or that the complete internal plan has been released.

The short answer, with sources

  • Were public figures flagged? Yes. A March 23, 2025 DOJ-FBI email instructs reviewers to flag other prominent names for further review.
  • Were those names automatically hidden? The released correspondence does not establish that. The referenced spreadsheet and portions of the approved review plan were not released.
  • How can readers verify it? Open the official DOJ 41-page FOIA PDF, then compare FBI Vault Part 13 and the plain-English claim check.
Source / legal distinction: This is a 41-page DOJ Office of Information Policy interim FOIA release containing emails dated March 18–27, 2025. It is not the October 9, 2026 court order, and the review predates the November 2025 Epstein Files Transparency Act.

March 23: reviewers instructed to flag additional public figures

PDF p. 2 (Bates 25-cv-3200_002) contains messages from the Office of the Deputy Attorney General and FBI about training and list updates. A March 23 8:14 a.m. message references an attached spreadsheet labeled Public Release List 2025.03.23 0810.xlsx and asks that reviewers flag other possible public figures beyond the first list for contemporaneous review. An earlier 7:44 a.m. message likewise calls for additional prominent names encountered in the field to be flagged. A 7:21 a.m. FBI reply says that the DOJ list would be incorporated into training under a category identified as “5.”

The word flag matters. The record describes escalation to a review process; it does not say that appearance on a list alone required suppression, or establish what happened to each flagged name. The named spreadsheet is referenced but not reproduced in the 41-page PDF.

March 22: DOJ-approved project scope, but withheld operative text

PDF pp. 5–9 preserve a March 22 evening exchange describing DOJ approval of a revised project plan, training materials and processing guidance. The project plan's operative passages are substantially withheld under FOIA exemption (b)(5). Staffing, coordination and training activity are visible, but the full criteria cannot be reconstructed from the partial release.

That distinction is important for accountability: the PDF provides some originating correspondence previously missing from GAH's FBI Vault Part 13 source chronology, but not the full approved training manual or complete decision rules. It is not a basis to assert concealed misconduct.

What the victim-redaction emails actually instructed

PDF pp. 24–25 and 37–38 describe handling of victim and third-party details. The correspondence instructs reviewers to protect identifying information associated with victims, including information that might indirectly reveal their identity, and to seek contextual review for certain materials. A task-force message describes compiling a combined list from victims, family members, witness-related material and other investigative records so identifying people would not inadvertently expose victims.

A March 23 exchange uses a figure of 1,114 names, while the FBI Vault's earlier chronology describes a list containing 1,115 names. We have not reconciled dates, versions or deduplication rules for the two figures. Neither number is evidence of a list of clients or perpetrators.

The emails document intended protections and processing questions; they do not, by themselves, prove the accuracy of every later redaction.

A separate October 9 court order: redacted handwritten FBI notes due October 16

In Phang v. Blanche, No. 1:26-cv-01417, Judge Emmet G. Sullivan signed ECF 57 on October 9, 2026. The order finds DOJ's explanations adequate for several challenged victim-name redactions and discharges related show-cause matters, including a separate issue involving an unavailable set of handwritten notes.

For three other FBI interview reports—EFTA01245620, EFTA02858481 and EFTA02858491—DOJ supplied unredacted underlying handwritten notes but not the requested redacted versions. The court directed DOJ to submit those redacted notes to the judge for private, in-camera review by noon Eastern on Friday, October 16, 2026. This is not an order to release the notes publicly by that deadline.

The court order does not itself adjudicate the separate certified administrative-record index or the reported ECF 51 deadline dispute. We have not independently verified a later ruling on those matters.
