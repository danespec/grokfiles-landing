The Barak Record, 2010–2018: What the Scheduling Files Actually Show

Seven source records spanning eight years show a recurring logistics channel around Jeffrey Epstein and Ehud Barak: proposed meetings, multi-day calendar entries, a proposed telephone call, and invitations routed through assistants and household contacts. The records establish repeated scheduling activity. They do not, by themselves, establish attendance for every proposed event, the substance of meetings or calls, political influence, agency, illegal activity, or misconduct.

Executive finding

The durable finding is a recurring scheduling channel—not a conclusion about what happened inside the meetings.

HIGH confidence: the seven cited records contain the scheduling, calendar, call-coordination, and invitation text described below. LIMITED: whether every proposed meeting occurred and what was discussed. NOT ESTABLISHED BY THESE RECORDS: political influence, agency, representation, illegal activity, or misconduct.

Public context for the 2010 record

Israel's Knesset biography records Ehud Barak's service as prime minister from 1999 to 2001 and his later government and ministerial roles. In December 2010, Brookings' Saban Forum record lists Barak, then Israel's defense minister, among participants in the Dec. 10–12 forum in Washington.

That context matters because the earliest source below references a Washington trip for the Saban conference. It corroborates the event reference; it does not prove that a separate Barak–Epstein meeting occurred.

The source-locked chronology

2010 — a proposed meeting around Saban travel

EFTA00559539, dated Nov. 11, 2010, is a scheduling chain with Nili Priell. The message asks whether the visit is official or unofficial, whether “Mr. Barak” will have time to meet Epstein, and offers private space for a possible “four-eyes” conversation. Priell's messages also refer to Washington for the Saban conference and then New York.

The source supports the existence of that scheduling discussion. It does not independently establish that the proposed meeting occurred or what any private conversation contained.

2012 — a proposed roster grows around a Barak/Summers meeting

EFTA00404605 preserves a Sep. 23–24, 2012 chain with Priell. One message says Epstein is to meet with Barak and Larry Summers and asks about time and location. The chain then proposes or adds Jes Staley, Andrew Feldstein, Reid Weingarten, Ian Osborne, and later Joe Pagano at specified times.

This is a strong logistics record and a weak attendance record. It shows proposed scheduling and a changing guest list; it does not establish that every proposed guest attended or what any meeting was for.

2013 — two different scheduling records

EFTA00389664 is a multi-day schedule dated Jun. 3, 2013. It lists a 4:00pm appointment with Barak on Jun. 3 and a noon lunch with Barak and Bob Kerrey on Jun. 4. A schedule entry is evidence that an event was placed on a calendar, not independent proof of attendance.

A separate Sep. 3 record, EFTA02574127, shows Epstein proposing Barak “on the 22?” along with Joshua Cooper Ramo. Olivier Colom replies that meeting both would be welcome and asks what the options are for Barak. Again, the record supports planning, not completion.

2014 — an invitation to join a Barak visit

EFTA00366797, dated Jul. 15, 2014, shows Lesley Groff asking Kathy Ruemmler whether she might “come and sit with Ehud Barak” on Jul. 17 while Barak was expected at Epstein's home. The same chain also discusses a separate lunch and an earlier request to speak “re Gates.”

The Barak invitation is explicit. Ruemmler's attendance at that proposed visit is not established by this email chain, and the record does not establish the content or purpose of any Barak discussion.

2016 — arranging a direct call

EFTA00322817, dated Jul. 8, 2016, shows Groff asking Priell to arrange a call because Epstein wanted to speak with Ehud. Priell proposed 2pm New York time and asked which number; Groff replied that Ehud could call Epstein's private line.

This record establishes contact coordination. It does not establish that the call connected, how long it lasted, or what was said.

2018 — a dinner invitation names Barak and Bannon

EFTA00473701, dated May 7, 2018, shows Groff inviting Noam and Valeria Chomsky to a May 12 dinner and saying that Steve Bannon and Ehud Barak would be there.

An invitation is not an attendance sheet. The record does not independently establish that Barak, Bannon, or the Chomskys attended, nor does it establish discussion content, agreement, political coordination, or misconduct.

What changes when the records are read together

No single scheduling message carries much interpretive weight. Together, however, the records establish that Barak-related contact was not confined to one isolated archive hit. The surviving source set spans 2010, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2016 and 2018 and repeatedly routes logistics through Epstein's staff and Priell or through Epstein's own schedules.

That supports a narrow finding of recurring contact and scheduling infrastructure. It does not answer the more consequential questions—what was discussed, why contact continued, what role any intermediary played, or whether any political or business outcome followed. Those questions require different evidence.

Presence standard. A name on a schedule, a proposed guest list, or an invitation is evidence of what the document says. It is not automatically evidence of attendance, influence, agreement, agency, motive, liability, or guilt.

Two archive corrections came out of this source reopen

The deeper source pass corrected the public Barak archive itself. First, EFTA00389664 had been summarized as a Wed. Jun. 5, 2013 noon Barak entry. The OCR instead shows a Jun. 3 4pm Barak appointment and a Jun. 4 noon lunch with Barak and Bob Kerrey. Second, EFTA00404605 had been left as an undated open receipt slot; its OCR source-locks a Sep. 23–24, 2012 scheduling chain.

Those corrections are being published with this investigation so the article and underlying archive use the same record chronology.

What remains open

  • Which proposed meetings in these chains were actually attended?
  • What was discussed in any meeting or call that did occur?
  • Can independent records establish purpose beyond logistics?
  • Can the September 2012 proposed roster be corroborated as actual attendance?
  • Can the May 2018 dinner invitation be corroborated with an attendance record?
  • Do other records close the gap between scheduling access and any claimed political, legal, or business consequence?
