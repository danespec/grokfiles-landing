Executive finding

The public record proves a records-access dispute. It does not prove adjudicated obstruction.

New Mexico DOJ's acquired June 30 letter documents a sequence of requests for unredacted Epstein/Zorro Ranch records, including a May 3 Touhy request and a July 31 requested access date. The official DOJ-side public record acquired for this run is not a written NM-specific response; it is Jay Clayton's July 15 Senate exchange saying the documents are subject to protections including court protective orders and that DOJ personnel were seeking an accommodation. NMDOJ letter Clayton exchange

The strict classification is unresolved. July 31 is a New Mexico demand and denial-trigger date in the acquired letter, and separately an interim-report date for the New Mexico House Special Investigatory Committee under HR 1; no acquired source makes July 31 a federal court production deadline. NMDOJ letter HR 1

What is disputed or not public

NMDOJ's position is that unredacted access has not been granted and that the delay is unreasonable. DOJ's public position located here is that protections, including court protective orders, constrain access and that an accommodation was being explored. Those statements are not automatically contradictory because a protective-order negotiation can coexist with a pending or incomplete production record. NMDOJ release Clayton exchange
