The GADECO Deal Did Not End with Barak's November 3 Withdrawal

A primary-source reconstruction corrects an artificial endpoint: the record continues through November 24 with a conditional fee instrument and additional buyer diligence.

Grok Archive Hub Editorial Desk · October 12, 2026 · Documentary evidence audit

Chronology correction. Barak withdrew from an oil-company introduction on November 3, 2014, but later correspondence shows renewed discussions. Neither the final sale nor any payment to Barak has been verified.

October 25: Epstein conveys interest

Jeffrey Epstein told Ehud Barak that Leon Black wanted information about an oil-company opportunity sent for review. Barak said he wanted to speak to Epstein first. This does not show Black received company documents or entered a transaction.

November 3: an actual withdrawal

Barak told GADECO owner Jack Grynberg he had reconsidered helping to sell the company. He said he had spoken with contacts in the energy business but had not transmitted the provided material to them. The subsequent correspondence means that this withdrawal cannot be treated as the final record.

November 18: a conditional finder-fee instrument

A two-page proposal transmitted in the Grynberg correspondence bears apparent handwritten corrections and Grynberg's signature. Its commercial term was a 3% enterprise-value fee contingent on transaction closing and receipt of proceeds, payable to Barak's Hyperion company for introducing the buyer. The visible document does not show a countersignature by Barak; the signature has not been forensically authenticated.

November 19–24: a Chinese consortium

In a November 19 message, Barak told Grynberg he had sent a company overview and résumé to a prospective buyer. By November 24 he was still discussing the contingent fee terms while representatives of a private Chinese consortium considered the proposal. The Chinese consortium should not be assumed to be Leon Black or his representatives; the archive does not establish that connection.

The proposed fee and the failed buyer discussion

The November correspondence separates an introduction from an earned commission. A percentage stated in a proposed fee instrument describes what the parties were discussing, while its closing-and-proceeds condition identifies an event still needing proof. Later negotiations therefore correct the idea that November 3 ended the record, but they do not reverse the separate finding that no completed fee event has been verified.

The Chinese consortium’s refusal adds a second endpoint: a prospective buyer stopped advancing the proposal. Its reasons concerned historical earnings disclosure and valuation. Neither the consortium correspondence nor the renewed fee discussion establishes that another buyer subsequently closed. The records would need to be joined through a signed transaction agreement, identified purchaser, closing statement and payment trail before the proposed commission could be described as paid.

Why the consortium declined

A Chinese consortium declined to advance a proposed $1.6 billion acquisition of oil interests associated with Jack Grynberg's GADECO operation in November 2014. Correspondence reviewed by Grok Archive Hub identifies two reasons given by consortium representative Joachim Chao on November 27: requested three-year profit-and-loss statements had not been supplied, and he considered the price excessive amid declining oil prices.

The seller had provided a September 2014 group pro forma statement, but it did not supply the three years of historical company earnings the buyer wanted. Grynberg favored inspection of assets in Denver and argued that old results were less useful for valuing a growing operation. In a December 3 message, after Chao's refusal, he questioned the buyers' seriousness. These are differing commercial assessments; the correspondence does not establish deceit.

An engineering report described prospective reserve cash flows as unrisked and dependent on future drilling. Its forecast assumed realized oil prices near $93 per barrel for 2015; by November, the U.S. Energy Information Administration forecast 2015 West Texas Intermediate crude around $78. Those are not identical price measures, but the shift underscores the valuation uncertainty.

A separate $1.6 billion estimated affiliate-sale receivable in the pro forma has not been traced to a named affiliate. Matching amounts do not prove a completed sale or accounting misconduct.
