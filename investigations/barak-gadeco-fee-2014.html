Why the Chinese Consortium Declined the GADECO Deal: Missing Accounts and a $1.6 Billion Valuation

The 2014 Chinese acquisition proposal ended after a dispute over requested historical accounts and valuation. A recovered pro forma statement and petroleum-engineering forecasts now explain what the buyer was shown—and why a $1.6 billion asking price was difficult to evaluate.

Grok Archive Hub Editorial Desk · First published October 12, 2026 · Expanded source audit October 12, 2026

Principal finding: Joachim Chao's consortium informed Ehud Barak on November 27, 2014 that it would not proceed, citing the absence of three years of requested profit-and-loss statements and a price it considered too high amid falling oil prices. Grynberg's later criticism of the buyer came after this refusal. The reviewed records do not establish a completed sale or a paid success fee.

What this investigation adds to previous reporting

Al Jazeera's December 9, 2025 investigation already reported Barak's GADECO brokerage work, the proposed $400 million for 25% or $1.6 billion for 100% terms, a possible 5% commission, and the Chinese proposal ending without a deal. GAH does not present those facts as new. The additional contribution is a direct comparison of the late-November buyer/seller correspondence with the original financial and petroleum-engineering exhibits. It shows exactly which documents were available, which were requested, and which assumptions underlay the proposed price.

What the seller called a financial statement

The six-page PDF dated September 30, 2014 is labeled a pro forma statement of assets and liabilities. It covers Grynberg Petroleum, RSM Production and multiple affiliates—including GADECO and Pricaspian. It is not a GADECO-only set of 2011–2013 income statements. It lists approximately $4.518 billion in group pro forma assets, not independently appraised or audited GADECO sale proceeds.

Two entries are particularly material. One is a reported $998.13 million receivable associated with an estimated sale value of interests in oil and gas properties; the other is a $1.6 billion receivable described in Note 2 as management's estimated value on a sale of an affiliate. Together those entries amount to roughly 57.5% of the group pro forma asset total. The document does not identify the affiliate underlying that latter figure or provide a completed sale, settlement receipt, or purchaser payment record. An estimated management value is not proof of realized cash or a collectible trade debt.

The rest of the packet includes historic BG Group/Pricaspian correspondence and forward-looking Karachaganak settlement projections that expressly disclaim a warranty or guarantee of forecast accuracy. Those forecasts are not GADECO's historical earnings, and should not be added to its estimated company value.

Important distinction: the broader seller packet did include some historic well-level data, including actual 2013 lease-operating costs and product prices. These are useful engineering inputs, but they do not replace the consolidated three-year profit-and-loss statements, cash-flow reconciliation or entity-specific diligence Chao requested.

A possible rationale for the $1.6 billion asking price—not verification of it

GAH recovered an eight-page report titled GADECO Grand Total Summary Reserve Report — Smith, June 1, 2014, prepared by consulting petroleum engineer Stephan Smith, P.E. It labels the economic estimates unrisked projections, subject to revision. It calculates future net cash flows under different discount rates:

Engineering forecast, June 1, 2014Present-worth estimate
Undiscounted projected net cash flows$11.295 billion
12.5% discount rate$2.018 billion
15% discount rate$1.483 billion
20% discount rate$825.7 million
Company's proposed sale price (separate executive summary)$1.600 billion

The proposed company price falls between the report's 12.5% and 15% discounted estimates. That is a potential valuation rationale, not a documented pricing formula. The engineering net-present-value model is not the same as an arm's-length valuation of an entire legal entity: working capital, third-party debt, title, taxes, and the rights and obligations included in a sale could differ.

Its forecast anticipated growth from approximately 180 modeled wells in 2014 to 2,061 in 2028 and assumed 2015 realized oil prices of roughly $92.73 per barrel. Both are assumptions about future performance, not proof those wells were drilled or those prices collected. Some listed wells belonged to outside operators, with GADECO holding fractional interests rather than full ownership.

Producing wells versus wells still on the drawing board

A September 3, 2014 production-and-drilling schedule reports approximately 10,089 barrels of oil equivalent per day from existing production. Its higher 22,589 BOE/day figure combines producing wells with estimated production associated with drilling and planned wells. Calling the latter figure “current production” would be misleading.

The source's printed gas-to-BOE conversion also requires checking: its existing-production total is compatible with a roughly 6 Mcf-to-one-BOE factor, but applying that same factor to the combined gas and oil totals yields approximately 24,281 BOE/day rather than the printed 22,589. The schedule mentions gas flaring; without its original calculation workbook we cannot determine whether this difference reflects exclusions, varying conversion conventions or a numerical error. It is an unresolved arithmetic question, not proof of manipulation.

An earlier January 2014 reserve breakdown separately differentiates proved developed producing (PDP) from proved undeveloped, probable and possible forecast categories. Its report date and scope differ from the June grand total. Combining or substituting these forecasts without a same-date reserve reconciliation would double count or misclassify potential value.

Why the oil-price objection mattered in November

The consulting engineer's June model predated the late-2014 crude-price decline. The U.S. Energy Information Administration's November 12, 2014 outlook projected average 2015 WTI crude prices at roughly $78/barrel and Brent at about $83/barrel, after lowering its prior outlook. These are market benchmarks and forecasts, not GADECO's precise realized wellhead price; simply substituting them into a model would require explicit differentials, tax, cost and development assumptions. Nevertheless, the dated EIA release independently substantiates why a bidder might question the attractiveness of an earlier, higher-price forecast.

Grynberg's position also deserves accurate treatment: he argued that expanding assets should be evaluated through field inspection and current prospects rather than through older accounting returns. That is a negotiating position, not inherently improper. Chao's demand for operating history and valuation support was likewise conventional due diligence. Their correspondence documents an unresolved commercial disagreement, not established deception by either party.

Separate fee instruments do not equal money received

One scanned buyer-side instrument dated November 17 appears to bear Joachim Chao's signature and contemplates 2% of project enterprise value if his consortium invested or completed a purchase. Its apparent signature is not forensically verified and the reviewed scan does not show Barak's countersignature. Barak said on November 19 that he intended to countersign. A separate Grynberg-side November 18 proposal contemplated a 3% closing-contingent introduction fee for Hyperion, associated with Barak; the parties also disagreed over some triggering terms. These are possible opposing-party success-fee structures, not a verified 5% liability or payout. Nothing in this review establishes a final acquisition or earned commission.

The unresolved $1.6 billion accounting entry

The most specific remaining missing record is the supporting schedule for Note 2 of the September pro forma statement: it needs to name the affiliate associated with the estimated $1.6 billion sale receivable and identify its underlying proposed transaction. The same nominal price appears in GADECO's 100% cash-sale proposal and near one engineering valuation scenario; matching amounts alone do not establish that these are one transaction, that an accounting standard was breached, or that a buyer owed the money. A ledger entry, board-approved sale agreement, or auditor/accountant workpaper would be required.

For legal context, a Colorado Court of Appeals ruling in 2015 discussed earlier voidable-transfer findings affecting GADECO and other Grynberg entities, while a 2018 Colorado Supreme Court opinion addressed a later family corporate-control and privilege dispute. Neither case is a judicial finding that this specific 2014 consortium offer was fraudulent, that Chao acquired GADECO, or that the disputed estimated receivable represents the same sale.

Editorial conclusion and source boundaries

Concluded for this source set: Chao's Chinese consortium decided not to advance the proposed acquisition on November 27. The source trail documents a missed financial-disclosure expectation, an escalating oil-price mismatch, contingent brokerage contracts and valuation materials that relied on future drilling. It does not document completed consideration, a paid finder fee, or the accounting origin of Note 2's $1.6 billion estimated receivable.

The private source set includes contemporaneous emails, engineering reports and images of the six-page pro forma and fee instruments.
