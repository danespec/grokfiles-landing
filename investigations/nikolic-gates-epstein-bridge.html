The Nikolic Bridge: From Gates’s First Epstein Dinner to the 2013 Exit Negotiations

The surviving record now joins the introduction, the first dinner and the later exit negotiations without treating Epstein’s self-addressed drafts as verified fact.

Grok Archive Hub Editorial Desk · October 6, 2026

Core finding. Two source layers now join cleanly. A January 2011 Nikolic–Epstein email schedules a 7:30 meeting and says Nikolic will come with “bill”; Epstein’s Jan. 31 schedule names Bill Gates and Boris Nikolic for dinner; and Gates told House Oversight in 2026 that Nikolic arranged his first dinner with Epstein at Epstein’s New York home. Two years later, House exhibits show Epstein and Nikolic discussing Nikolic’s departure from Gates’s organization. Gates testified that Nikolic ultimately told him Epstein was involved in those exit discussions. None of this establishes that Nikolic conspired with Epstein to entrap Gates; Gates expressly rejected that theory.

January 30, 2011: the first-meeting logistics

In House Oversight record HOUSE_OVERSIGHT_030887, Boris Nikolic, identified in the email header with bgC3, asks Jeffrey Epstein whether 7:30 the next evening works and says he will come with “bill.” Epstein replies that 7:30 is good. The same chain contains Epstein’s earlier suggestion that “the three of us” first spend time together before widening the social circle.

Standing alone, the lowercase first name would be an identity problem. The surrounding record resolves it. Epstein’s Jan. 31 schedule names a 7:30 dinner with Bill Gates and Boris Nikolic. Gates then testified to House Oversight that his first Epstein dinner occurred in January 2011 at Epstein’s New York home and that Nikolic had suggested and scheduled the dinner.

Gates’s 2026 account confirms the bridge

Asked how he first came to dinner with Epstein, Gates said Nikolic, who worked for him, told him he might be interested in dining with Epstein. Gates later said that for the dinner itself the attendees were Gates, Nikolic and Epstein. In another section of the interview, Gates identified the date as January 31, 2011.

Gates’s retrospective description of Nikolic’s role is unusually useful. He said he had understood Nikolic to be someone who had met Epstein first and was “kind of organizing the visits that took place.” After reviewing the later-released emails, Gates said he was surprised to see that Nikolic’s relationship with Epstein was more extensive than he had known at the time.

June 2013: Epstein enters the exit discussion

House Oversight marked EFTA00873874 as Gates Majority Exhibit 1. The Committee described it as a June 16, 2013 email chain between Epstein and Nikolic. The chain included a draft communication written from Nikolic’s perspective concerning his departure and proposed that Epstein oversee or negotiate on Nikolic’s behalf.

The draft used expansive language about Epstein acting as a financial and legal representative and about the confidentiality of communications. Gates told the Committee he had never received a message like that and had not been told that communications with Epstein were privileged. He also noted that Epstein was not a lawyer. This is a critical source boundary: the draft documents what Epstein and Nikolic were discussing, not what Gates received or accepted.

July 2013: self-addressed drafts are a different evidence class

The Committee separately entered EFTA01966988 and EFTA01967528, July 18 emails Epstein sent to himself that appear to draft language from Nikolic’s perspective. They contain much more aggressive claims and proposed economic terms. Gates said he had not seen those emails before their public release and disputed multiple factual assertions contained in them.

An Epstein-to-himself draft is evidence that Epstein created the text. It is not, without another receipt, proof that Nikolic authored every statement, that Gates received it, or that the allegations inside it were true.

What crossed from draft to real-world involvement?

One part did. Gates testified that Nikolic eventually told him he was involving Epstein in the discussion of Nikolic’s exit. Gates also described a later Seattle meeting in which Epstein participated in those discussions. Gates said he did not change his offer and characterized Epstein’s language as veiled rather than a direct threat.

The evidence therefore supports a narrower but stronger finding than the viral versions: Epstein was not merely name-dropped in private drafts. Gates’s own testimony places Epstein inside the later exit-negotiation process. But the testimony also rejects the proposition that every draft demand became an agreed term.

The conspiracy question was asked—and Gates rejected it

A House member directly asked whether Gates had come to believe Nikolic and Epstein had conspired before the 2011 dinner to draw him into Epstein’s orbit. Gates answered that he did not believe that happened and said he did not think Nikolic was part of the broader scheme described in the question.

Boundary. The documentary bridge proves introduction, meeting coordination, continuing Nikolic–Epstein communications and later Epstein involvement in Nikolic’s exit talks. It does not prove a preplanned conspiracy, criminal conduct by Gates or Nikolic, or the truth of every allegation contained in Epstein’s self-addressed drafts.

What this investigation does not claim

  • It does not infer criminal conduct from meetings, introductions or employment negotiations.
  • It does not treat Epstein’s self-addressed drafts as verified statements of fact.
  • It does not claim Gates received the June or July draft language that he testified he never saw at the time.
  • It does not convert sensitive personal allegations in draft text into adjudicated facts.
  • It does not claim Nikolic participated in a preplanned conspiracy; Gates’s testimony rejects that proposition.

Why this moves the record forward

The useful advance is the join. The 2011 email no longer has to be read as a free-floating “bill” reference: the schedule and Gates’s testimony resolve the meeting identity. The 2013 drafts no longer have to be read as proof that their proposed language was sent: Gates says it was not. At the same time, Gates’s testimony independently confirms the material point that survives both corrections—Nikolic brought Epstein into his eventual exit discussions.
