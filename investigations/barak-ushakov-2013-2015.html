Barak's 2015 Putin Meeting Request: An Official Refusal, Not a Completed Appointment

The contemporary Ushakov-office reply narrows an often overread diplomatic trail: Barak requested a meeting, received a refusal for the proposed dates, and tried to keep the request open.

Grok Archive Hub Editorial Desk · October 12, 2026 · Documentary evidence audit

Documented outcome: In June 2015 Barak requested an appointment with President Putin through Yuri Ushakov's office. The request was declined for the proposed St. Petersburg dates. A completed 2015 meeting is not established.

June 3–5, 2015

Barak requested a short appointment during the economic forum. On June 4, Ushakov's chief of staff Alexey Shilin replied that the president's schedule was full and requested alternative visit dates. On June 5, Barak asked that the request remain open. The direct evidence is a request, an explicit scheduling refusal, and a follow-up.

What remains open

The original 2013 meeting claim has not been independently confirmed with official schedules or participant records. A meeting request is not evidence that a meeting occurred. The source audit preserves that distinction.

Earlier reporting

The underlying 2013 channel was described in prior reporting by Drop Site News. GAH does not claim first publication of that relationship. The 2015 messages add an explicit refusal, narrowing what can be inferred about the later proposed meeting.

Separate suggestions

Released record EFTA01975333 preserves Epstein's suggestion that Barak ask whether a private conversation might be possible. It does not document a result. GAH finds no source connection to the February 2016 White House telephone call.
