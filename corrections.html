Corrections and Source Tips

Send durable corrections, source tips, broken-link reports, or archive questions. The archive improves by keeping source limits visible.

Safety copy: Do not send illegal material, private personal data, passwords, or anything requiring secure legal handling.

What to send

Correction requests

Send the page URL, the sentence or link at issue, the durable source that supports the correction, and a short explanation of the requested change.

Broken-link reports

Send the route, the broken source link, and any archived or official replacement if one exists.

Source tips

Send public, lawful, durable source pointers such as court records, official reports, public filings, or clearly provenance-tracked archive material.

What not to send

  • Do not send illegal material.
  • Do not send private personal data or credentials.
  • Do not send medical, legal-sensitive, or private identifying material unless it is already lawfully public and directly relevant.
  • Do not send unsupported allegations, threat labels, or identity guesses.

How corrections are handled

A correction can downgrade, caveat, relabel, or clarify a page. The archive favors narrow, source-linked changes over broad narrative claims. If a source gap remains unresolved, it should stay visible as an open receipt slot rather than becoming a finding.

What makes a correction actionable

The fastest correction packet includes four things: the exact public route, the sentence or source link that is wrong or incomplete, the replacement source, and the narrow change requested. For an archive dossier, include the EFTA identifier, page number, footer number, render filename, or PDF page reference if those are visible. For a methodology or policy page, point to the rule that is unclear and describe the reader risk created by the wording.

Source tips are handled differently from correction requests. A tip may create a review queue item, but it does not change a public claim until the source can be inspected, attributed, and placed into the right lane. If the tip is only a screenshot, copied text, or an unsourced assertion, it may be useful as a pointer but not as a citation. Durable sources include court dockets, official reports, public filings, agency pages, preserved source renders, and archive records with stable identifiers.

Possible review outcomes

  • Correction: the page said something wrong and the text, title, description, link, or source note is changed.
  • Clarification: the original sentence was defensible but too easy to overread, so a limit or example is added.
  • Downgrade: a claim loses confidence and moves to a review lead or open receipt slot.
  • Retirement: a weak route is removed from the sitemap or marked noindex while the underlying source remains accessible.
  • No change: the challenged text is supported by the cited source, but the request may still be recorded for future review.

Public notes and preservation

Grok Archive Hub does not delete source evidence to make a correction look cleaner. When the problem is interpretation, the visible page should change. When the problem is a generated route, search treatment can change while the archive record or PDF remains available. When the problem is OCR debris, the fragment should not remain an indexable entity or topic page, but the source location that produced it should stay traceable if it matters to the audit trail.
