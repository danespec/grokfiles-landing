Safety disclaimer: This page explains the redacted-files lane. A redaction is a document condition, not proof of what is underneath it. Redacted text, missing pages, withheld names, or obscured sections do not prove guilt, motive, coordination, concealment, or misconduct by themselves.

Redaction map

Reader Summary

Redactions are document conditions. They can show that something is withheld or obscured, but they do not prove what is hidden. Missing context becomes an open receipt slot.

Redacted files need source path, page context, visible surrounding text, redaction type if known, and source-chain review. Public redaction receipt cards will come later only after review.

A redaction is not a conclusion. It is a visible condition of a record that needs source context, page context, and careful caveats before it can support any public finding.

What Redactions Can Show

Withheld or obscured text

A redaction can show that text was withheld, obscured, blacked out, degraded, or otherwise unavailable in the visible record.

Unavailable names or sections

A redaction may show that a name, section, page, attachment, or field is unavailable without proving what is underneath it.

Page context

Surrounding visible text, headers, footers, page numbers, and nearby sections can help frame what the redaction is and is not.

Document condition

Redactions can describe record condition, completeness, degraded pages, partial records, or source-chain gaps.

Possible withholding context

Privacy or legal withholding may be present, but the archive does not guess the hidden content.

Source checks

Redactions can trigger related document checks, source-chain review, missing-page review, and open receipt slots.

What Redactions Do Not Prove

  • A redaction does not prove guilt.
  • A redaction does not prove misconduct.
  • A redaction does not prove motive.
  • A redaction does not prove coordination.
  • A redaction does not prove what words are underneath it.
  • A missing page does not prove concealment by itself.
  • A black box is not evidence of a specific hidden fact.
  • A redacted file does not replace court, DOJ, official, or source-linked records.

Redaction Review Workflow

  1. Step 1Redacted file/source is identifiedThe review starts from a source file, archive record, or documented redaction condition.
  2. Step 2Source path and page context preservedPage/file reference, headers, footers, visible surrounding text, and source-chain context are kept together.
  3. Step 3Visible surrounding text reviewedThe review describes what remains readable without guessing hidden text.
  4. Step 4Condition described without guessingThe record may be blacked out, missing, degraded, withheld, partial, or unclear.
  5. Step 5Related archive IDs checkedRelated source records are linked only when the connection is supported.
  6. Step 6Missing material stays openMissing pages, missing attachments, degraded links, and unavailable context become open receipt slots.
  7. Step 7Privacy and legal-sensitive material protectedPrivate personal data and legal-sensitive material are not exposed.
  8. Step 8Only reviewed items become future cardsOnly safe, source-linked items may become future redaction receipt cards.

Future Redaction Receipt Schema

No redaction receipt ID is assigned until the item passes review.

Identity and source

Visible context

Caveat fields

What the redaction does not prove, related archive IDs, and open receipt slots.
