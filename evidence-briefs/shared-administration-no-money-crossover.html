Evidence Brief · Financial records · Negative finding · Source-locked

Same Office, Same Bank Relationship, Different Money Rails

A cross-domain review found overlapping administrators and the same Deutsche Bank wealth-management relationship on both Southern Trust's technology-investment side and the 2006 Butterfly Trust side. It did not recover the same account, the same transaction-originating entity, or a traceable flow of technology-investment proceeds into Butterfly beneficiary payments.

Executive finding

Shared administration is supported. Material money crossover is not.

The strongest supported overlap is people + bank relationship: Darren Indyke and Richard Kahn appear in both administrative domains, and Deutsche Bank KYC identifies the Southern Financial Relationship and relationship manager Stewart Oldfield. The recovered records do not support upgrading that overlap into a shared account or traced funds path.

These pages show the technology wire, the Butterfly legal instrument, and the regulator’s aggregate description. They support overlap at the administrative/bank-relationship level without supplying a transaction bridge.

Two different rails

The technology-investment rail is centered on Southern Trust Company. A Deutsche Bank wire record at EFTA01376049 identifies Southern Trust's money-market account ending 9244 and a $20 million May 31, 2016 wire to Honeycomb Partners. A separate Southern Trust memorandum at EFTA01404004 directs a $1 million March 2015 wire to ERGO Ltd. with the reference “Reporty investment.”

The beneficiary-payment rail is legally and operationally distinct. The 2006 Butterfly Trust agreement names Jeffrey Epstein as grantor and Darren Indyke and Richard Kahn as trustees. Deutsche Bank material at EFTA01376144 identifies a Butterfly deposit account ending 0552. The suffix does not match Southern Trust's 9244 account.

What the beneficiary-wire aggregate actually says

The New York Department of Financial Services record at EFTA00151495 states that Epstein used the Butterfly Trust account and various other accounts to send more than 120 wires totaling $2.65 million to Butterfly beneficiaries. The stated purposes included hotel expenses, tuition, and rent.

That wording matters. The regulator describes a mixed-account, mixed-beneficiary aggregate. It does not say every wire originated from Butterfly, and it does not classify every beneficiary as a victim.

An FBI Senior Financial Investigator email at EFTA00037690 separately says the trust “seems to be a conduit” for payments to female victims. The same record expressly warns that the underlying SAR information contains unsubstantiated allegations. The native flow-of-funds presentation and FinCEN spreadsheet named as attachments have not been recovered in the archive.

The shared people are real

The overlap is strongest at the administrative layer. EFTA01404004 is a Southern Trust memorandum for the $1 million Reporty investment with “From: Darren.” The 2006 Butterfly instrument names Darren Indyke and Richard Kahn as trustees. Kahn also appears throughout the technology-investment work as an investment and wire-operations contact.

That makes Indyke and Kahn dual-domain administrators. It does not establish that either person moved technology-investment proceeds into a Butterfly beneficiary payment. The row-level human signer for most beneficiary-wire entries is not recovered.

The shared bank relationship is real too

Deutsche Bank KYC at EFTA01281966 names the Southern Financial Relationship and relationship manager Stewart Oldfield. Run 11's account reconstruction places both Southern Trust and the 2006 Butterfly relationship within that broader Deutsche Bank servicing structure.

But a relationship umbrella is not an account. The directly recovered account records remain distinct: Southern Trust *9244; Butterfly *0552.

The transaction search stops short of crossover

The recovered Butterfly transaction extract at EFTA01282041 was searched for Southern Trust, Honeycomb, AdFin, Reporty, ERGO, and Valar. None of those technology-rail names appears in that extract. The same review found no recovered Southern Trust → Butterfly wire and no recovered Butterfly → Southern Trust wire.

A May 24, 2018 Honeycomb Ventures return of $1,752,515.84 into Southern Trust is documented, but no onward wire from that return into Butterfly was recovered. Months-later Butterfly activity cannot be converted into a transaction chain by chronology alone.

Important limitation. The banking record is incomplete. Some statement periods are partial; the native FinCEN spreadsheet is missing; older JPMorgan wire workbooks remain unusable. “Not recovered” therefore means exactly that—it is not proof that no transfer could ever have occurred outside the surviving record set.

What this brief does not claim

  • It does not claim that shared administrators imply shared purpose.
  • It does not classify every Butterfly beneficiary as a victim.
  • It does not merge the 2006 Butterfly Trust with the separate 2013 Butterfly Trust.
  • It does not characterize the documented overlap as money laundering, conspiracy, facilitation, or layering.
  • It does not turn an incomplete-record negative finding into proof that a transfer never happened.

Why a negative finding is worth publishing

The records support a tempting visual pattern: the same office personnel, the same private bank, the same relationship manager, and large movements of money in adjacent years. The transaction evidence does not support the stronger conclusion that the two rails merged. Publishing that boundary is part of the investigation.
