The NG911 Email: Planned FCC Meetings, No Proven Access Chain

A November 23, 2015 email shows Jeffrey Epstein forwarding Ehud Barak a public Tom Wheeler article about modernizing 911. Barak replied by describing Reporty sales activity and prospective U.S. contacts, including a tentative meeting with Wheeler and a planned meeting with Henning Schulzrinne. The recovered thread does not show Epstein arranging either contact, does not prove either meeting occurred, and does not establish a causal path to Carbyne's later U.S. deployments.

Executive finding

The email documents prospective outreach, not completed government access.

The strongest supported chain is public NG911 article → Epstein forwards URL → Barak reports sales/investor activity and prospective FCC contacts. The archive does not supply a meeting confirmation, advisory-board acceptance, FCC endorsement, procurement award, or later-customer referral arising from this thread.

The controlling NG911 email

The linked PDF viewer is the controlling public receipt; OCR and image renders remain secondary aids.

15:34 UTC: Epstein forwards a public article

The cleanest copy of the thread is EFTA00672765. At 15:34 UTC on November 23, Epstein sends Barak a New York Times URL for Tom Wheeler's NG911 opinion piece. The preserved message contains the URL and standard footer text; it does not contain an introduction to Wheeler, a meeting request, contact information, or a note that Epstein had spoken with the FCC chairman.

A duplicate production copy, EFTA02372377, preserves the same exchange with noisier OCR.

16:31 UTC: Barak describes what Reporty was already doing

Fifty-seven minutes later, Barak replies that the article is “fascinating” and says Reporty personnel had met in Barcelona with Microsoft, Siemens, GE and IBM, as well as what he calls the CTO of New York City and a senior homeland-security official from India.

Those are Barak's reports inside the email. The thread itself does not identify the individual corporate attendees, name the New York City official, or supply independent meeting records. They are therefore preserved as reported activity rather than upgraded to independently confirmed meetings.

Wheeler: “Probably I have to meet”

Barak then writes: “Probably I have to meet with Wheeler (FCC Head).” Tom Wheeler was serving as FCC chairman in 2015. The grammar matters: this is a prospective statement, not a report that a meeting had happened or a calendar confirmation.

The bounded Run 2 search did not recover a follow-up record confirming that Barak or Reporty met Wheeler. That is an archive result, not proof that no meeting could have occurred outside the surviving record set.

Schulzrinne: planned meeting, wrong title

Barak also says he was going to meet the next day with Columbia computer-science professor Henning Schulzrinne, whom he describes as the FCC CTO “or something similar,” and adds: “We want to put him on our advisory board.”

The public role history corrects the title. Schulzrinne had served as FCC Chief Technology Officer through August 2014; from September 2014 through August 2016 he was an FCC Technology Advisor. Columbia's later appointment notice says Scott Jordan was the sitting Chief Technologist until the end of 2016.

The recovered corpus does not show completion of the proposed November 24 meeting or Schulzrinne accepting a Reporty/Carbyne advisory role. The statement therefore remains planned meeting + intended advisory recruitment.

What Epstein's role is—and is not—in this thread

Inside this specific exchange, Epstein's documented act is narrow: he forwards Wheeler's public article. The contacts Barak discusses appear in Barak's reply; Epstein does not introduce Wheeler, Schulzrinne, the corporate names, the New York City official, or the prospective investors in the preserved thread.

That does not erase Epstein's separate investment relationship with Reporty documented elsewhere in the archive. It means only that this email should not be used as evidence that he brokered FCC access.

2018 U.S. deployments do not close the 2015 gap

Carbyne's August 2, 2018 company announcement says its c-Lite NG911 plugin was live in the United States and names Fayette County, Georgia and Ocean County, New Jersey as early adopters. The announcement describes c-Lite as a plugin for legacy 911 call-handling systems.

That rollout occurred roughly 33 months after the November 2015 email. Run 2 did not recover a documentary chain connecting Wheeler, Schulzrinne, the Barcelona contacts, or the unnamed New York City official to those county deployments. Chronology alone is not a referral chain.

What this brief does not claim

  • It does not claim Barak or Reporty actually met Tom Wheeler.
  • It does not claim the planned Schulzrinne meeting occurred or that he joined the advisory board.
  • It does not treat Epstein's forwarding of an article as an introduction to government officials.
  • It does not infer improper influence, favoritism, lobbying success, or government endorsement.
  • It does not attribute the 2018 Fayette/Ocean deployments to the 2015 FCC contacts without a documentary bridge.
