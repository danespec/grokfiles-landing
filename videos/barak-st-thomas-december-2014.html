Original YouTube title and published description

Ehud Barak's Island Weekend — and the Flight Ticket in Another Man's Name

In December 2014, Jeffrey Epstein invited former Israeli prime minister Ehud Barak and his wife Nili to his Caribbean island. The public story has always been that Barak's island visit was brief and all business. The documents tell a longer story. "Come to Carib?" — Epstein, 2:27 AM, December 6, 2014. Told entirely from the primary record — the Baraks' own email, Epstein's office files from the DOJ release — this video walks the full timeline: the invitation, Nili's flight proposal (American Airlines 1275 into St. Thomas, Dec 12), Epstein's same-night approval, his office asking in writing whether to book the hotel under the name "Ehud Barak" — "or do you go by something else?" — the room booked under Nili's name instead, four days hosted on St. Thomas with lunch on the island, simultaneous invitations to Larry Summers and Tom Pritzker, and the staff farewell: "Mr. Epstein truly enjoyed this weekend." And then the anomaly: the only flight itinerary for that trip anywhere in Barak's own email names a different passenger — COHEN/RONEN MR, on the Baraks' exact flight, arriving at the exact proposed minute, First class home to Tel Aviv (PNR 7HLSOQ). "Ronen Cohen" appears nowhere else in seven years of Barak's email, or in 1.38 million released DOJ documents. This video presents both readings of that ticket — and labels them as readings. The documents are the story. Document receipts and their limits

  1. Barak leak id 18414

    Nili and Epstein invitation and early return-plan thread, December 5–6, 2014. The thread records plans, not completed travel.

  2. Barak leak id 18310

    Nili’s December 7 St Thomas email proposes AA 1275 into St. Thomas on December 12 at 12:55 PM and a December 15 return.

  3. Barak leak id 18018

    Epstein accepts the proposed visit and assigns Lesley to coordinate. Approval is not evidence of boarding.

  4. Barak leak id 18243

    Hadassim’s Trip Details: TEST booking email. The subject anomaly does not identify the traveler.

  5. Barak leak id 19004

    Headerless COHEN/RONEN MR itinerary, PNR 7HLSOQ, AA ref YGVYVP. Inbound matches Nili’s proposal; the return booking leaves December 14. A ticket is not a boarding record or an authenticated alias identification.

  6. Barak leak id 15729

    Nili writes that they are in the Caribbean on December 13, 2014.

  7. Barak leak id 14998

    February 2, 2015 exchange asks about Barak’s St. Thomas bodyguard; the named person is Jarrod Nadel. This does not exclude a second officer.

  8. DOJ EFTA00356456

    December hotel, arrival and farewell correspondence. The hotel-name exchange chooses Nili’s name; December 12 arrival, island-lunch arrangements and December 15 farewell establish the visit. Hotel naming does not establish an airline alias.

  9. DOJ EFTA02596796

    Epstein invites Tom Pritzker: ehud on island for weekend, come visit. An invitation does not establish Pritzker attended.

  10. DOJ EFTA02346686

    Epstein’s Summers island-weekend invitation. It does not establish Summers attended that weekend.

  11. DOJ EFTA02300238

    November 30 Hoffman/Ito departure logistics. Island-operations context, not evidence of attendance during the Baraks’ visit.

Leak IDs identify a separate mailbox source family. They are labeled rather than assigned invented public document URLs. An archive locator is not itself authentication of every statement in a document.

What this does not prove

The completed December 12–15 visit is documented. Cohen = Barak is Thomas’s unproven hypothesis; a real associate traveling in parallel is the alternative. The return dates differ, and the records do not establish boarding under another identity, criminal conduct, or attendance by Summers or Pritzker. Corpus-wide negative searches are findings attributed to the supplied research scope, not proof that no such person exists.

Open questions and records still needed

  • Hadassim’s account and payment records for PNR 7HLSOQ and the TEST booking.
  • An arrival-party or passenger record that joins the itinerary to an independently established traveler identity.
  • Independent whereabouts records for December 12–14, and any changed or unused return reservation.
