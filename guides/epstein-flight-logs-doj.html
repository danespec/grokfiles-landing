Epstein flight logs: find the actual DOJ Maxwell trial exhibits

Flight-log screenshots travel farther than their citations. The most reliable starting point is the government's own release index for the documents identified as Flight Log_Released in U.S. v. Maxwell. The records are flight or pilot-log material, not a verified master manifest of every journey or passenger.

Seven official government PDF links

DOJ listed a base flight-log exhibit and Parts 1–6 in its February 27, 2025 first-phase release. These are the file links published by the agency, not GAH-generated mirrors:

The agency's link text and records are the published primary-source identifiers. GAH does not assert that every year from 1997 through 2006 is covered, or that these files are a complete aviation history.

How to interpret a log entry responsibly

  1. Identify the correct kind of record. A pilot's flight log may record aircraft registration, airports, date or flight time. It may differ from a full passenger manifest, ticketing record or an air-traffic-control movement report.
  2. Track each PDF and page. Cite the agency's exact link and the page where a handwritten entry appears. For reproducibility, keep the retrieval date, page count and SHA-256 digest. Never stitch snippets from separate logs into a single apparent journey.
  3. Resolve leg dates and locations. Handwritten digits, ditto marks, ICAO/IATA airport codes and image quality can make optical character recognition unreliable. Verify a leg against its surrounding pages before assigning a date, route or person.
  4. Separate a name from conduct. A logged name, initials, staff reference or ambiguous abbreviation is not proof a person knowingly participated in trafficking, visited an island or committed any offense.
  5. Check completeness before making negative claims. A person or flight absent from these government PDFs might appear in other books, aircraft or periods, or be hidden by illegible handwriting or missing portions. No match is not proof a flight never happened.

How this relates to the frequently cited 1997–2006 date range

Searches commonly request Epstein flight logs for 1997–2006. That search phrase is a research window, not a verified statement about the exact temporal coverage of this seven-PDF DOJ release. To claim a complete year-by-year dataset, a researcher must inventory every source page, validate dates and aircraft, and document omissions. GAH has not certified that comprehensive coverage.

DOJ's disclosure was an official 2025 publication of records associated with the Maxwell trial; it did not itself adjudicate the meaning of each log entry or implicate every individual mentioned. Another public court exhibit may reproduce a different portion of aviation records and must be treated as a separate source.

Evidence boundary: A flight leg is a transportation record, not evidence of a crime. Don't publish contact details, victim information or sweeping claims about named passengers from scraped/OCR excerpts. Preserve source, context and limitations.

Other source types

The same DOJ announcement separately lists a redacted contact book. It is not a flight manifest, and a contact entry cannot establish a flight. See also how to verify a DOJ record and the corrections policy.
