Epstein's 2007 draft federal indictment: locating the 56-page document

The missing document behind many searches for a “56-page Epstein indictment” has a concrete release identifier: EFTA01703108. A public repository describes a 56-page scan of an unfiled 2007 federal draft and attributes its source to DOJ Data Set 10. That is a released draft, not evidence of a filed federal indictment or a criminal conviction.

Why 53 pages, 56 pages and 60 counts are different figures

56 pages

The length shown by the Commons file record for the released draft labeled EFTA01703108. This identifies a PDF edition, not necessarily the only draft prosecutors produced.

53 pages

Older court filings and DOJ Office of the Solicitor General material refer to a 53-page proposed indictment. We have not established that it is page-for-page identical to the 56-page scan.

60 counts

DOJ OPR describes a proposed 60-count indictment supported by an 82-page May 2007 prosecution memorandum. A charge count is not the page count of an indictment.

The dates, revision histories, pagination and count descriptions may reflect different drafts or how particular filings characterized them. The official materials cited here do not by themselves prove how those versions relate. Do not splice their figures together as though they came from a single certified court filing.

What was—and was not—filed

Drafting proposed federal charges is a prosecutorial step, not the same as filing an indictment returned by a grand jury. The 2007 negotiations led to a federal non-prosecution agreement in September 2007, and Epstein subsequently entered Florida state guilty pleas in June 2008. The latter are separate proceedings. See the dated non-prosecution-agreement chronology for the sequence and primary citations.

Some copies of the draft contain redactions. We are not reproducing redacted names, private contact details or alleged victims' identifying information here. Mention in a charging draft is not proof of guilt, and a draft count is an allegation rather than a judicial finding.

How to verify the source responsibly

  1. Start with the DOJ Data Set 10 release and locate identifier EFTA01703108. DOJ may require adult age verification for the document.
  2. Record the edition and its page count from the document you actually opened. A third-party file listing is useful for discovery but does not authenticate its contents against DOJ's current server bytes.
  3. Distinguish the document text from the 2020 DOJ OPR executive summary and the Solicitor General filing, which describe prosecutorial history rather than substituting for the draft itself.
  4. Record unresolved discrepancies explicitly. To certify that a 53-page description and this 56-page scan are successive revisions, obtain the original versions, their creation or transmission dates, and a verified document comparison.
Evidence limit: A 56-page released scan is now publicly identifiable. This does not establish that its page sequence matches an earlier 53-page draft, that all related documents were disclosed, or that anyone mentioned in the draft committed the alleged offenses.
