Epstein's “black book”: finding the official redacted contact book

By Grok Archive Hub · Published October 13, 2026

A searchable name or phone entry in a contact book is not a finding about the person named. For research, start with the version and source of the document—not a viral screenshot, a private address dump or a separate manuscript that happens to use “black book” in its title.

Primary released document

Agency: U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ), Office of the Attorney General.

Official release: February 27, 2025, first phase of declassified Epstein files.

Document's official label: C. Contact Book_Redacted.

How to verify the PDF before interpreting it

  1. Check the source domain. The official publication is under justice.gov. A third-party image or extracted text can omit pages, change cropping, introduce OCR errors or combine releases.
  2. Check the exact label. DOJ calls this file Contact Book_Redacted. A version marketed as “uncensored” or “full black book” may be a different and independently unverified copy.
  3. Preserve version metadata. Note the official source URL, retrieval date, whether the PDF was updated, page count and local SHA-256 if your research requires a reproducible snapshot. Avoid posting personal phone numbers or addresses from the file.
  4. Read contacts as contacts. An entry may reflect a business number, employee, family member, public office, outdated address, directory import or a real relationship. A name alone does not establish a meeting, flight, crime or knowledge of crimes.
  5. Corroborate before writing a finding. Match an independently dated email, flight manifest, financial record, sworn evidence or court decision before making even a limited claim about actions.

What this government release establishes

The February 2025 DOJ announcement establishes that the department itself published a redacted contact book as one component of its first-phase release. It does not establish that the file is a complete historic archive of every address book, that all entries remain accurate, or that named people participated in any wrongdoing. The DOJ announcement also separately lists flight-log exhibits; the two record types should not be conflated.

Privacy and public-interest boundary: Do not scrape, republish or contact private individuals based on directory entries. GAH intentionally provides the official redacted government file and a source-verification method rather than exposing people's contact information.
