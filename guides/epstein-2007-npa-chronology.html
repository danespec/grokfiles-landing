Epstein's 2007–08 non-prosecution agreement: what the dated records establish

The sequence matters. A proposed federal indictment, an offer to resolve the case in state court, a signed federal non-prosecution agreement and the later state guilty plea were different steps on different dates. This guide follows official DOJ findings and a federal appellate decision, with unresolved documentary questions kept visible.

At a glance: Federal prosecutors developed a proposed case in 2007, signed an NPA on September 24, 2007, and Epstein did not enter his state guilty pleas until June 30, 2008. DOJ's 2020 Office of Professional Responsibility (OPR) review criticized the resolution and victim notification decisions while distinguishing poor judgment from a finding of attorney professional misconduct. Read the official OPR executive summary (PDF).

Dated sequence from the primary record

2005–July 2006 · local and federal investigations

Palm Beach investigation and FBI referral

The Palm Beach Police Department began investigating in 2005. A Palm Beach County grand jury indicted Epstein on a state solicitation charge in July 2006. Police dissatisfaction with that outcome led to an FBI referral and a federal investigation. These are stages of the investigative history, not federal convictions.

Source: DOJ OPR Executive Summary, pp. i–ii.

May 2007 · proposed federal charges

A draft indictment, not a filed indictment

An Assistant U.S. Attorney submitted a proposed 60-count indictment and a supporting prosecution memorandum to supervisors. The OPR summary describes the proposed count total; it does not give its page count. A separate DOJ Supreme Court brief refers to a 53-page draft indictment. The separate DOJ locator EFTA01703108 identifies a 56-page scan, but its byte-for-byte original, version relationship to the 53-page description, and filed-versus-proposed status remain a distinct authentication question. The draft must be verified before GAH offers an authenticated original PDF.

Sources: OPR Summary, p. i; DOJ Office of the Solicitor General brief, p. 6.

July 31, 2007 · initial federal proposal

The state-based resolution discussed

At a meeting with defense counsel, federal prosecutors offered to conclude the federal investigation if Epstein pleaded guilty to state charges, agreed to at least two years in custody, registered as a sex offender and accepted a mechanism for civil recovery by victims. The subsequent negotiated agreement would differ in its sentence recommendation.

Source: OPR Summary, p. i.

September 24, 2007 · signed federal NPA

A federal promise not to prosecute in the Southern District of Florida

The agreement required state guilty pleas and an agreed sentencing recommendation of 18 months in county jail followed by 12 months' community control. In return, the federal office would forgo its case against Epstein and specified or potential co-conspirators in that district, subject to the agreement's terms. The OPR summary says victims were not consulted before the NPA was signed. Signing the NPA was not the same event as the later guilty plea.

Sources: OPR Summary, pp. i–ii; Eleventh Circuit, Doe No. 1 v. United States, 749 F.3d 999.

June 23, 2008 · DOJ leadership review

Deputy Attorney General declines intervention

After months of defense attempts to change or invalidate the negotiated arrangement, the Office of the Deputy Attorney General told defense counsel it would not intervene. This was an internal federal review of the agreement, not a new charge or acquittal.

Source: OPR Summary, p. ii.

June 30, 2008 · state guilty pleas

State convictions and sentencing

Epstein pleaded guilty in Florida state court to felony solicitation and procurement of minors to engage in prostitution. The court imposed consecutive jail terms totaling 18 months followed by community control. This is the state criminal judgment; it should not be described as a federal conviction under the proposed 2007 indictment.

Source: OPR Summary, p. ii.

July 7, 2008 · victims challenge

Crime Victims' Rights Act proceedings begin

A victim filed an emergency petition alleging that federal prosecutors had violated her rights by resolving the case without consulting affected victims. The OPR review and later federal court litigation address distinct legal questions; a finding about notification rights must not be conflated with the later OPR professional-discipline standard.

Source: OPR Summary, pp. ii–iii.

What the later official review concluded

In November 2020, DOJ publicly described OPR's findings. OPR did not find professional misconduct by the reviewed DOJ lawyers under its disciplinary standard. It did find that victims were not treated with the expected forthrightness and sensitivity, and concluded that former U.S. Attorney Alexander Acosta exercised poor judgment in pursuing the NPA and failing to ensure victims received notice of the state plea. A negative disciplinary finding does not erase the review's criticism. DOJ's November 12, 2020 statement.

The signed agreement, its exhibits and the later court outcome

Original agreement length: In the published April 15, 2021 en banc In re Courtney Wild decision, the Eleventh Circuit describes the agreement signed on September 24, 2007 as a seven-page NPA (Judge Hull dissent, judicial opinion p. 102). The majority describes a 53-page proposed indictment in its account (p. 4). These are different documents and different counting units. A PDF that bundles an NPA with later addenda, exhibits or cover pages need not have seven total PDF pages. Read the court-published en banc opinion (PDF).

Two different legal rulings: The Southern District of Florida found in February 2019 that prosecutors violated victims' rights to confer and be treated fairly; the en banc Eleventh Circuit later denied the CVRA mandamus petition in April 2021, holding that the Act did not permit enforcement through the victims' freestanding civil action when the government had filed no federal criminal charges. The 2021 court explained the procedural ground while recounting the earlier district-court decision; it was not a new finding that an NPA was a federal conviction. See the majority opinion pp. 2–3 and its summary of the lower court on p. 109.

Signed scan authentication still open: A government-hosted court-record locator identifies an attachment in Government of the United States Virgin Islands v. JPMorgan Chase Bank (S.D.N.Y., 1:22-cv-10904), Document 239-19, filed July 25, 2023. See the DOJ court-exhibit locator. Its public path currently redirects to a DOJ age-verification page when checked from this audit environment. We therefore have not authenticated its full PDF bytes, signature pages, attachments or exact relationship to other copies; an indexed excerpt is only a source lead. It cannot be represented as the original signed seven-page PDF until the actual document is lawfully retrieved and compared.

Document-status boundary: This page is a dated synthesis of official DOJ reports and a published court decision. It is not a claim that GAH has authenticated every signed original, the complete investigative file, the 2007 indictment draft, or all victim notifications. The draft was a proposal; the NPA was an agreement; and the June 2008 plea resulted in state convictions.

For the now-identifiable 56-page scan labeled EFTA01703108, see the separate draft-indictment source guide. Its relationship to earlier descriptions of a 53-page draft is not authenticated.
