Operating rule

A ledger, not a verdict.

The purpose is to preserve tension in the public record. An entry identifies the claim being tested, the record that supports or limits it, what the record leaves silent, and the narrow confidence that can be assigned.

How to read

How an entry is adjudicated

Each lane answers a different question. None may silently substitute for another.

01

Claim lane

The proposition being tested is stated narrowly enough to be proved, limited, contradicted, or left unresolved by identifiable records.

02

Record lane

The source chain identifies what each document actually establishes. A route, source card, or search result supports only the narrow proposition attached to it.

03

Source first

Original editorial work must expose the relevant record, date, provenance, extraction risk, alternate reading, and evidentiary limitation.

04

Silence stays visible

Where the linked sources do not establish motive, purpose, conduct, identity, continuity, legal status, or causation, the ledger leaves that gap open.

FARA Review Signals Are Not Legal Conclusions

A contradiction-ledger investigation explaining how the Barak FARA review route should label review signals without alleging violations.

Published

Status

Claim under review

Review signals or investigative flags establish a legal conclusion.

Record position

The linked investigation treats those signals as prompts for review and expressly avoids converting them into an allegation or adjudicated violation.

What remains silent

The cited route does not independently establish motive, legal status, criminal conduct, or a final agency determination.

Narrow finding

The records support a review signal. They do not, without additional authoritative evidence, support a legal conclusion.

Correction standard: stronger primary records, a documented legal determination, a broken citation, or an overbroad inference can change this entry.
