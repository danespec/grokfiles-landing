House Judiciary Democrats release 53-page Trump–Epstein staff report: what is documented, alleged and still open

On October 26, 2026, Democratic staff of the House Judiciary Committee published a 53-page report examining allegations connecting Donald Trump to Jeffrey Epstein's abuse and trafficking network. The report synthesizes accounts involving 11 women, including five accusations of rape or sexual assault, and calls for an independent special counsel. These figures describe what the report says; they are not GAH findings that the alleged conduct was established.

Read the primary record: House Judiciary Democrats — complete staff report (PDF, 53 pages) · Official October 26 announcement.
Publisher: House Judiciary Committee Democratic staff, under Ranking Member Jamie Raskin. This is a partisan staff analysis, not a bipartisan committee finding, a criminal indictment or a judicial decision. The report includes descriptions of sexual violence. GAH does not reproduce victim-identifying information.

What was published—and when

The October 26 release is a new congressional staff document. The underlying accounts and purported evidence largely concern historical conduct in the 1980s and 1990s; some cited interviews, FBI forms and records were disclosed earlier. The document's date is not the date of any new criminal charge, court ruling or completed government investigation.

1. Eleven accounts assembled into one staff argument

Report PDF pages 3–4 and 17–36. Democratic staff distinguish five direct sexual-assault allegations, three accounts the staff interprets as attempted or possible introductions for sexual activity, and three further women discussed in associated records. These groups have different evidentiary footing and cannot be treated as eleven adjudicated offenses. Some statements were made to investigators or in litigation, others through staff interviews or referenced reporting. No independent criminal finding is made in this document. See the report's summary.

2. Modeling-world and travel-record connections are contextual evidence, not proof of a conspiracy

Report PDF pages 9–16 and 37–50. The staff report assembles modeling-industry contacts, contemporaneous press accounts, witness statements and references to flight logs, and disputes past public descriptions of the Trump–Epstein relationship. Even where an underlying event such as a documented association or flight can be authenticated, it does not by itself prove participation in abuse or trafficking. GAH has not separately reauthenticated every exhibit and cross-reference within the report.

3. DOJ handling and Maxwell-interview conclusions are interpretations, not court findings

Report PDF pages 5–7 and 36–51. Democratic staff accuse DOJ and FBI officials of withholding evidence, terminating inquiries and limiting a line of questioning during a 2025 interview with Ghislaine Maxwell. The staff characterize this as possible obstruction. Those characterizations are allegations requiring the underlying interview transcript, disclosure decisions, applicable privilege or victim-protection rules, and any judicial findings. The report itself does not establish criminal obstruction or a legally operative withholding judgment.

4. The final recommendation seeks a special counsel

Report PDF pages 51–53. Democratic staff recommend appointment of a special counsel under 28 C.F.R. § 600.1 and increased cooperation with state and foreign investigators. A request for appointment is not evidence that a special counsel has been appointed, that a grand jury has charged anyone, or that a prosecution has begun. Read the recommendation in context.

Competing positions and evidentiary limits

Committee Democrats: argue that survivor accounts and FBI records justify independent criminal investigation and that the government's disclosure and investigative decisions warrant scrutiny. That is the position of the report's authors, not a court finding.

Trump administration: President Trump has denied wrongdoing and knowledge of Epstein's abuse. In its January 30, 2026 official release statement, DOJ said that some public-submitted Epstein materials include false allegations against Trump, defended its withholding categories, and described its disclosure as compliant with the transparency law. In reporting published October 26, a White House spokesperson again said Trump had been exonerated and cited the administration’s document releases. These statements are the administration's position—not independent adjudications resolving each witness account or disclosure dispute.

What GAH still needs before drawing stronger conclusions

  • A crosswalk from each quoted interview and FBI FD-302 to an original public identifier, complete document, redaction version and date—not just a staff-report quotation.
  • Responses and rebuttals attached to each distinct allegation; gaps in public evidence are not automatically evidence of concealment.
  • Any subsequent signed judicial orders, formal agency decisions, or new congressional compulsory process, each clearly separated from staff advocacy.
