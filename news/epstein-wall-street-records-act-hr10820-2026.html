Bipartisan Lawmakers Introduce Epstein Wall Street Records Act

H.R. 10820, introduced October 15, would open federal bank-regulator files and require review of possible Epstein-related financial violations. It is a proposal, not enacted law.

Grok Archive Hub News Desk · Published October 16, 2026 · 2-minute read

Lawmakers led by Senator Elizabeth Warren and Representative Robert Garcia announced the bipartisan Epstein Wall Street Records Act on October 15, 2026. The House version, H.R. 10820, was introduced by Garcia that day and referred to the House Financial Services Committee. Senators Ron Wyden and Richard Blumenthal and Representatives Thomas Massie, Ro Khanna and Raja Krishnamoorthi were among those named in the legislative announcement.

The introduced House bill focuses on supervisory records held by financial regulators concerning institutions with known or alleged Epstein connections. The Federal Reserve, FDIC, OCC and SEC are among the agencies named in the announcement. The sponsors say the bill would require disclosure of specified supervisory material and investigation of potential banking, securities, or illicit-finance violations.

The Senate Banking Committee minority office also released correspondence from federal financial regulators and banks, including Barclays, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan Chase. The existence of those responses and requests is verifiable; the sponsors' characterizations of inadequate answers are their advocacy positions, not findings of unlawful conduct.

Legal status: introduction and House committee referral are legislative steps. The Act has not been shown enacted or signed into law. Investigations, prohibitions, civil penalties, industry bars and criminal referrals discussed in the proposal are potential future consequences subject to its enactment and applicable procedures, not consequences already imposed by this bill.

Evidence boundary: No criminal guilt, executive misconduct or unlawful bank transaction is established merely by a congressional bill, a regulator inquiry, a bank reply, or an Epstein-related name appearing in supervisory records.
