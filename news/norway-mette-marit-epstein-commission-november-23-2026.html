Norway’s Queen Declines Epstein Commission Request: What the Files Actually Show

Norwegian reporting says the Royal Court declined the Epstein commission’s request to speak with Queen Mette-Marit on November 23, following constitutional advice from the Prime Minister’s Office. A separate, limited review of released Epstein records identifies evidence of social proximity and reported contact, not evidence of criminal wrongdoing by the Queen.

News and official context

VG, November 23: Royal Court refusal, including Prime Minister’s Office response to NRK · Aftenposten, November 23: NRK-originated report · Official commission mandate · Royal Court: current title and succession

Why now: a request for a conversation was declined

According to VG and Aftenposten, drawing on NRK reporting on November 23, the Royal Court declined an October request from the independent commission. The Prime Minister’s Office advised against the conversation on constitutional grounds, citing longstanding practice and separation of powers. That is the reported executive advice, not a judicial ruling on the commission’s powers. Commission chair Amund Djuve said the Queen could have valuable information; the commission is considering its next steps.

The official commission mandate records the Storting’s March 17 decision to establish an inquiry; the Storting’s first-meeting notice records its April 15 appointment and the January 31, 2028 final-report deadline. The inquiry concerns matters raised by the release of the Epstein files, including Norwegian connections and the conduct of Norwegian authorities. The Royal Court’s biography confirms that Mette-Marit became Queen on August 28, 2026 when Haakon VIII succeeded King Harald V. Earlier documents use the title Crown Princess.

The November refusal does not erase earlier public reporting about her relationship with Epstein. Norwegian reporting also describes her March 2026 NRK interview. This article does not independently reconstruct that interview or her full contact history.

Scope and source method

Ren’s November 25 corpus.db review identified 13 distinct exact-phrase matches and three additional title-only documents. The loose search returned 22 rows, including nine false positives from stemming and OCR noise. One email was released under two Bates numbers. The reviewed set contains 17 corpus records representing 16 distinct documents. The seven December 2019 FBI files form one correspondence chain, not seven independent confirmations.

The News Desk independently checked the November news reports and official institutional context. The document-level findings below are attributed to Ren’s supplied review; the News Desk has not independently reread the underlying PDFs. Bates identifiers are source locators, not assertions that a particular public DOJ download URL has been verified. This review is not a search of every possible Mette-Marit reference, alias, spelling or associated person.

What the reviewed records show

1. February 28, 2011 — a proposed introduction

EFTA02021990 (DS10). Boris Nikolic asked Epstein about availability around March 20 for a friend visiting New York. A Google image-search link for Mette-Marit is the basis for interpreting the unnamed friend as the then Crown Princess. The email does not establish that the proposed meeting occurred.

2. January 27, 2011 — secondhand regards from Davos

EFTA00648375 (DS9); EFTA01801291 (DS10). These are two releases of the same Lawrence Krauss email from Davos, relaying the Crown Princess’s reported regards to Epstein. They establish a third-party statement, not direct communication or a meeting between them.

3. February 11, 2013 — Epstein’s account of contact

EFTA00954360 (DS9). Epstein told Larry Summers that he had spoken to the Crown Prince and Crown Princess and said they would host Summers in Norway. This is Epstein’s own statement about contact, and the proposed hospitality concerns Summers, not Epstein. The reviewed set does not independently establish the conversation or that Epstein was hosted.

4. November 13, 2013 — a health-related news link

EFTA02373909 (DS11). Nikolic sent Epstein a news link about Mette-Marit and described communication with Haakon. The document describes Nikolic’s contact with Haakon; it does not show contact with Mette-Marit.

5. 2009–2014 — lists and a public-event program

EFTA00774846 (DS9), May 13, 2009: A list of UN goodwill ambassadors sent to Epstein includes Mette-Marit in connection with UNAIDS. Receiving a list is not evidence of contact with a listed individual.

EFTA02686105 (DS11), January 2011: A World Economic Forum participant list names Mette-Marit. Jeffrey Epstein is not listed; the only Epstein is David R. Epstein of Novartis. EFTA02707384 (DS11), January 2013: A later Davos participant list names Mette-Marit but does not list Jeffrey Epstein. These lists do not establish that they attended together, or resolve attendance outside the lists.

EFTA01204217 (DS9), June 10, 2014: A New York M·A·C Viva Glam Inspiration Gala program honors Mette-Marit. Epstein does not appear in the program. Its presence in the corpus is not evidence of his attendance or contact.

6. December 3, 2019 — the FBI responds internally to a press inquiry

DS9 correspondence: EFTA00147946, EFTA00147955, EFTA00147994, EFTA00147996, EFTA00147998, EFTA00148000 and EFTA00148002. A Norwegian journalist asked the FBI about anonymous-source newspaper reporting and whether the FBI wanted to speak with the Crown Princess. The FBI internal correspondence describes no known current interest in Norway or known connection to Norway or interest in the individuals at issue at that time. Copenhagen’s legal attaché reported that a Stockholm tip turned out to be from a reporter. The record does not support converting the newspaper report into a verified FBI pursuit of Mette-Marit. Nor does it prove that investigators never had any interest at other times. A separate person is redacted and remains unidentified here.

7. January 23, 2019 — a third party’s account

EFTA01027660 (DS9). A correspondent identified only by first name claimed a friendship with the Crown Princess and discussed potential collaboration. Epstein responded using Mette-Marit’s first name. The exchange establishes a third-party claim and Epstein’s familiarity with that name, not a meeting with Mette-Marit. No attempt has been made to identify the correspondent further.

What the reviewed records do not establish

  • No reviewed document alleges misconduct by Mette-Marit or establishes criminal wrongdoing by her.
  • A mention on a list or in a program does not establish contact; a proposed introduction and reported regards do not establish a meeting.
  • The 2013 hosting statement concerns Summers, not Epstein.
  • The 2019 FBI records concern a journalist’s inquiry; their recorded response is not confirmation of a pursuit of the Queen.
  • Haakon’s wider corpus footprint is separate from Mette-Marit’s, except where a specific document explicitly links them.
  • No message addressed directly to or sent by Mette-Marit appears in this reviewed set. That is a bounded observation, not a claim about the complete DOJ release.
  • The proposed March 2011 meeting, other reported encounters and the commission’s next steps are not established by these documents.

What to watch

Follow the commission’s response to the refusal, any further Royal Court or Prime Minister’s Office statements, and the official final report due January 31, 2028. A refusal to participate does not establish the inquiry’s eventual conclusions. See the GAH EFTA compliance tracker for the U.S. release context.
