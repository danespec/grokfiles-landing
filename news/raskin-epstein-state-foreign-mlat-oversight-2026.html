Raskin Seeks DOJ Records on Epstein-Related Foreign and State Investigations

The House Judiciary Committee’s ranking member is asking the Justice Department to account for evidence-sharing decisions affecting investigations beyond federal prosecutors. The documented action is an oversight request—not a finding of obstruction.

Grok Archive Hub News Desk · Published October 22, 2026 · Primary document signed October 21, 2026 · Four-page letter

Rep. Jamie Raskin, the ranking Democrat on the U.S. House Judiciary Committee, sent a four-page letter dated October 21, 2026, to Attorney General Todd W. Blanche and Vaughn A. Ary, director of the DOJ Office of International Affairs. A committee press release dated October 22 announced the request. The letter questions federal cooperation with domestic and overseas authorities investigating people linked to Jeffrey Epstein.

Its main news value is the scope of records requested, rather than its politically charged characterization of DOJ conduct. The letter demands information about both foreign mutual legal-assistance requests and state/local law-enforcement requests, providing a defined documentary test of what DOJ may later disclose.

The two categories of records requested

  1. International assistance: All records and correspondence concerning Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty (MLAT) requests submitted by foreign countries seeking DOJ assistance in investigations of potential Epstein co-conspirators. The request includes records identifying the people or investigations involved.
  2. Domestic cooperation: All records and correspondence concerning DOJ decisions to deny or oppose requests for documents, information, or other assistance sought by state or local U.S. law-enforcement bodies investigating potential Epstein co-conspirators.

These requests are set out on page 4 of the signed letter. Raskin asks for a response by 5:00 p.m. on November 4, 2026. Because the letter does not expressly name a time zone for the deadline, the time should not be presented as a court-enforceable Eastern-time filing cutoff.

Why New Mexico and the United Kingdom are mentioned

On pages 2–4, the letter cites New Mexico’s attempts to obtain unredacted federal investigative material and reports of a British mutual-assistance request associated with the investigation of former ambassador Peter Mandelson. It also refers to international investigative activity in Norway, Poland and Latvia. Those examples explain the oversight inquiry but do not independently verify that DOJ unlawfully denied assistance or establish the status of any foreign prosecution.

For New Mexico, Raskin cites the state’s complaint in New Mexico v. U.S. Department of Justice, D.D.C. 1:26-cv-02762, filed August 5, 2026, as support for state allegations concerning access to records. That case is a different action from New Mexico’s October 21 civil suit against estate executors, and its allegations should not be merged with findings from other proceedings.

The letter also references DOJ’s arguments regarding Privacy Act disclosure exceptions in the New Mexico records dispute. The agency’s legal position and the state’s claims must be evaluated from their pleadings and any eventual signed court ruling; the congressional letter does not settle that dispute.

What is established—and what is not

Established: The letter exists, is dated October 21, identifies Blanche and Ary as recipients, requests two defined categories of evidence-sharing records, and specifies a November 4 response deadline.

Not established: A judicial finding of obstruction; a new subpoena; DOJ’s receipt, agreement or production of the requested material; a verified tally of denied MLAT requests; or an adverse conclusion about an uncharged person merely associated with Epstein.

Next document checkpoint: A publicly posted DOJ response or a further authenticated congressional action after the requested deadline. The deadline itself is not evidence that documents were provided or withheld.

How the oversight request differs from Phang v. Blanche

The October 16 court-stamped filing in Phang v. Blanche (D.D.C. 1:26-cv-01417) concerns DOJ’s representation that it privately supplied redacted handwritten FBI notes for in-camera judicial review. The Raskin correspondence is a separate congressional request. Neither document, standing alone, resolves the Phang administrative-record index issue or establishes a judicial disposition of the government’s ECF 51 postponement motion.
