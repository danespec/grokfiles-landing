Why a Chinese Consortium Declined a Proposed $1.6 Billion Oil Deal

November 2014 correspondence and engineering forecasts explain the dispute over financial disclosure and price. A completed sale has not been established.

Grok Archive Hub News Desk · Published October 12, 2026 · About 2 minutes

A Chinese consortium declined to advance a proposed $1.6 billion acquisition of oil interests associated with Jack Grynberg's GADECO operation in November 2014. Correspondence reviewed by Grok Archive Hub identifies two reasons given by consortium representative Joachim Chao on November 27: requested three-year profit-and-loss statements had not been supplied, and he considered the price excessive amid declining oil prices.

The seller had provided a September 2014 group pro forma statement, but it did not supply the three years of historical company earnings the buyer wanted. Grynberg favored inspection of assets in Denver and argued that old results were less useful for valuing a growing operation. In a December 3 message, after Chao's refusal, he questioned the buyers' seriousness. These are differing commercial assessments; the correspondence does not establish deceit.

An engineering report described prospective reserve cash flows as unrisked and dependent on future drilling. Its forecast assumed realized oil prices near $93 per barrel for 2015; by November, the U.S. Energy Information Administration forecast 2015 West Texas Intermediate crude around $78. Those are not identical price measures, but the shift underscores the valuation uncertainty.

A separate $1.6 billion estimated affiliate-sale receivable in the pro forma has not been traced to a named affiliate. Matching amounts do not prove a completed sale or accounting misconduct.

The broader brokerage effort and unsuccessful Chinese proposal were previously reported by Al Jazeera in December 2025. This brief summarizes GAH's subsequent comparison of the original financial exhibits.

What remains unverified: No completed purchase or broker-fee payment was verified; the estimated receivable's underlying transaction remains unidentified.
