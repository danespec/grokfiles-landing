Epstein’s Death: What the Autopsy, EMS Records and Forensic Comparisons Establish

GAH compares Epstein’s autopsy and sworn testimony, a recovered EMS working copy, custody evidence and peer-reviewed forensic research. Suicide remains the best-supported explanation; material documentation gaps remain. Our documentary review neither changes the official determination nor establishes an alternative perpetrator. It identifies what the records show, what they contradict, and what remains unverified.

1. Custody: established failures, not established orchestration

The June 2023 OIG report (23-085, executive summary and chapters 5–7; archive EFTA00039025) documents the failure to replace Epstein’s transferred cellmate, falsified count and round records, excess linens and the DVR failure beginning July 29. No required rounds were performed after approximately 10:40 p.m. on August 9. The available common-area recording did not show anyone entering his tier during the overnight interval; it did not record his cell door. The OIG also describes witness accounts supporting the locked-cell chronology. It found no evidence contradicting the FBI determination of no criminality in connection with the death. That conclusion is not a direct recording of the death itself.

Two guards were charged with falsification, entered deferred prosecution agreements and had their charges dismissed after completing the agreements. The documented negligence is grave; it does not establish intentional facilitation or an order to kill. Read the existing MCC timeline and source-chain investigation for the underlying chronology.

2. The autopsy and the sworn interview do not fully agree

OCME case M-19-019432: Roman performed the examination on August 11, 2019. The report records hanging as cause and suicide as manner (EFTA00063519–20). It records fractures of the hyoid and both thyroid cartilage horns, wrist contusions, forearm abrasions, posterior neck muscle hemorrhage and deep left deltoid hemorrhage (EFTA00063522–25). Heart enlargement, hypertensive and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and fatty liver were also present. The report attributes rib fractures and liver injury to resuscitation.

3. The deltoid finding: the strongest countercheck

We tested the hypothesis that a shoulder muscle hemorrhage necessarily indicates an assault. The peer-reviewed German study by Schulz, Buschmann and colleagues (2011, International Journal of Legal Medicine 125:863–871, DOI 10.1007/s00414-011-0622-1) provides a direct counterexample. Its 62 suicidal hanging autopsies included 18 cases with internal muscle hemorrhages. Two had deltoid hemorrhages; neither of those two had undergone resuscitation. The researchers describe potential nontraumatic physiological mechanisms, including respiratory exertion and involuntary muscle activity.

The full text is accessible in Buschmann’s thesis, Table 1, p. 45 (journal p. 866); case numbers and disease/resuscitation fields are in the publisher’s supplemental dataset, p. 1. Two of 62 is approximately 3.2% in this particular cohort, not a population estimate. The comparison cases establish compatibility, not an individual diagnosis. They do not resolve the age of Epstein’s shoulder injury or establish an exact cardiovascular-disease match. Histology slides #9 and #10 and contemporaneous medical records merit independent review.

Neck fractures and internal muscle hemorrhage are not exclusive to homicide. The literature does not independently determine manner of death in an individual case. The autopsy, scene evidence, prior history and investigation must be evaluated together.

5. Who reviewed the medical findings?

Roman worked for NYC OCME. First Deputy Chief Medical Examiner Jason Graham authorized the family’s independent pathologist to attend and is named as reviewer on the signed final report (EFTA00063525, 63549–50). Chief Medical Examiner Barbara Sampson led OCME in 2019. Graham’s office-level review is documented; the internal review notes and any communications resolving the discrepancies are not in our verified set. The FBI recorded a November 14, 2019 conversation between prosecutors, Roman and OCME legal counsel (Serial 153, EFTA01688205; released FBI volume). That record precedes her May 2022 interview. The existence of counsel, supervision and prosecutorial contacts does not establish interference with a medical opinion.

The March 2022 OIG request sought the complete case file (EFTA00063517). The accompanying certification EFTA00063518 identifies the medical examiner file; the separate forensic biology file box is not checked. The autopsy lists fingernail and swab specimens, but the corresponding forensic biology results were not located in that file. NMS toxicology follow-up EFTA00063527–28 dated August 22, 2019, reports no tested synthetic cannabinoids above reporting limits; the pending notation in the earlier toxicology sheet is not an unresolved test result. Negative tests are bounded by their scope.

For institutional comparison, the Maryland Attorney General’s 2025 audit independently reviewed 87 selected restraint-related deaths, disagreeing with the original manner determination in 44 and unanimously favoring homicide in 36 originally non-homicide cases. This supports independent forensic oversight as a method, not an inference that the NYC office misclassified Epstein’s death. A medical manner classification is not a criminal verdict.

6. Identity, anonymous claims and FBI lead disposition

OCME’s August 11 case-management entries record a postmortem fingerprint submission and a matching response from New York’s criminal justice system (EFTA00063549); an identification entry also records family confirmation. These are strong evidence for identity, though GAH has not inspected the underlying biometric comparison images. No verified evidence establishes a body substitution or human cloning; research interests alone cannot establish either. We do not attribute an anonymous internet claim to a named individual.

The existing GAH 4chan attribution audit distinguishes the medical-information poster from a separate substitution allegation. EFTA00123462, EFTA00129268 and EFTA00133349 establish subpoena and provider-response records, not the truth of the allegations. The August 17, 2019 message (EFTA00129264, released-record transcription) directs recipients not to focus on the 4chan or IP-related subpoena records; its assignment context remains uncertain. In July 2020, prosecutors told defense counsel in United States v. Noel that the records obtained did not identify the author of the specified post (request 30, EFTA00023756–62; discovery response). This statement does not resolve every distinct post or later investigative step.

An OIG memorandum confirms that the FBI death investigation 90A-NY-3151227 was closed December 5, 2022 after the FBI found no criminality (EFTA00035805, released OIG closing memorandum). The memo identifies the closing communication as an attachment; it does not by itself supply every lead disposition. Do not confuse this case with the separate sex-trafficking investigation.

Conclusion: Suicide remains the best-supported explanation in the reviewed material. Severe custodial failures are established; a deliberate killing, staged death, political order or coercion of the examiner is not established. The documentation gaps warrant targeted independent review but do not supply affirmative evidence of an alternative conclusion.

Method and limitations: GAH reviewed supplied copies of the OCME autopsy and FBI file, the sworn interview, OIG report, CAD log and damaged FDNY report; compared published scientific records and historical FBI records; and did not examine original histology slides, the complete photographic set, original biometric comparison images or a complete authenticated FDNY file. No independent medical diagnosis is offered. Comparison cases were selected for relevance, not statistical representativeness. GAH will correct this review if better evidence emerges.
