Police Scotland Confirms Balmoral Inquiries Involving Epstein, Maxwell and Andrew

Police Scotland confirmed it has received information and is conducting active inquiries into the time Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor, Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell spent at Balmoral. The force describes the inquiries as being at a very early stage and has not identified any alleged offense, published the underlying information or announced charges.

Confirmed facts versus unresolved claims

What changed on October 30

The new development is official confirmation of active police inquiries about Balmoral, not a newly disclosed investigative report. Police Scotland says it received information relating to the presence of the three people at the royal estate in Aberdeenshire. The statement does not specify when that information was received, how it was obtained, or whether any formal criminal investigation or procedural step followed.

What the police did not say

Some outlets describe claims that women were trafficked to Balmoral. Those are reported allegations, not allegations confirmed by Police Scotland in this statement. A police spokesperson explicitly declined to provide investigative details. The absence of detail is not evidence confirming or disproving such claims.

Legal and evidentiary effect

A police inquiry can involve assessing information, contacting witnesses, or considering whether further action is justified. No Balmoral-specific arrest, charge, search warrant, formal finding, or court decision is established by the October 30 statement. Mountbatten-Windsor has denied wrongdoing relating to his Epstein associations. Mere presence, photographs and social contact do not establish individual criminal liability.

The timeline without conflating cases

  • 1999, reported: Photographs and later reporting place Epstein and Maxwell at Balmoral. A contemporaneous image is a historical source about presence, not proof of trafficking or other offenses.
  • Earlier 2026: Separate Thames Valley Police action concerned allegations of misconduct in public office. It must not be represented as a charge or finding in this Scotland/Balmoral matter.
  • October 30, 2026: Police Scotland publicly confirms receipt of information and active, early-stage inquiries. Nothing in the statement amounts to a judicial ruling.
