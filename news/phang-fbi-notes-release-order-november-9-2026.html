Federal Judge Orders Publication of Epstein FBI Handwritten Notes; Denies DOJ Delay

A federal judge has ordered the Justice Department to publish redacted handwritten FBI notes underlying three Epstein-related FD-302 interview reports. In a separate signed order, he rejected the government's request to postpone its certified administrative-record obligations. Neither order establishes that the underlying notes are already public.

1. What the judge actually ordered released

Judge Emmet G. Sullivan's November 9 order, ECF 64, states that he reviewed the materials DOJ submitted for the court's private in-camera examination on October 16 pursuant to ECF 57. This resolves the narrow question of whether the court had the materials for review: the judge expressly acknowledges reviewing them. It does not, by itself, establish that DOJ has published them.

The release order identifies handwritten interview notes that formed the basis for FD-302 reports bearing Bates numbers EFTA01245620, EFTA02858481 and EFTA02858491. DOJ must publish redacted versions in the Epstein Library in accordance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act and the department's January 4, 2026 attorney-review protocol.

The order gives DOJ fourteen days from November 9—a November 23 deadline absent a qualifying appellate stay—and directs a docketed notice of compliance within 24 hours of publication.

What the order does not do: it does not itself post the notes, release unredacted materials, establish a larger class of FBI notes must be published, or adjudicate any allegation in the FD-302 reports.

2. Why November 23 is conditional

ECF 64 expressly provides that if DOJ moves the D.C. Circuit for both a stay of this order and expedited review before the 14-day period expires, the publication order is temporarily stayed while the appeals court resolves that motion. A notice of appeal alone should not be reported as a granted stay under this specific clause.

Verification limit as of this publication: later docket mirrors list November 17 activity, including a notice of appeal and an administrative-record entry. Their underlying documents and the full appellate docket were not independently authenticated for this article. We therefore do not represent that the release deadline was stayed, that the notes have already appeared online, or that a deadline was violated. A later signed order or actual library publication would materially change the status.

3. Separate ruling: DOJ cannot defer the administrative-record list

In a distinct eight-page memorandum opinion and order, ECF 65, Judge Sullivan denied DOJ's ECF 51 request to postpone the certified list and production of the administrative record until after the Rule 12(c) motion. The court held that public availability of some DOJ records does not relieve the government of identifying the specific materials used in its EFTA compliance determination.

The judge ordered the parties to confer, directed DOJ to file a certified list of administrative-record contents by November 17, and ordered production of the administrative record to Phang by December 1. Separately, he extended Phang's response deadline on DOJ's Rule 12(c) motion to December 31.

Distinction: a certified list filed with the court is not the same thing as privately producing the underlying record to the plaintiff. The November 17 docket index is not a substitute for reviewing the contents of ECF 68. ECF 65 resolves whether ECF 51 was denied, but it is not a judgment on the merits of ECF 52.

5. What is still not established

  • Whether the November 17 notice of appeal includes an application meeting the ECF 64 conditions for a temporary stay.
  • Whether DOJ has actually published the ordered redacted handwritten notes or filed a notice of compliance.
  • The substance of the certified administrative-record list indexed as ECF 68, or whether its contents meet the court's instruction.
  • Whether the complete administrative record has been produced to the plaintiff; that obligation has a December 1 deadline.
  • The merits of the underlying Rule 12(c) motion, which the November 9 orders do not resolve.

These are verification limits, not findings of government misconduct or judicial approval. A rotating RSS feed or partial public docket mirror cannot prove a filing or private submission did not occur. Redactions and victim/witness privacy remain mandatory.
