FBI Vault Part 14: what the January 2026 Epstein-file review records actually show

On October 22, 2026, the FBI posted Part 14 of its Records regarding the review of the Investigative Holdings related to Jeffrey Epstein series. The 118-page PDF contains a January 5 internal message about Everlaw training and assignment uncertainty, and a January 4 DOJ attorney protocol for reviewing records under the Epstein Files Transparency Act. These are historical review-process records, not a new court ruling.

Primary records: FBI Part 14 — original 118-page PDF · Official FBI viewer · 14-part review-series index.
This is the review-holdings series, not the separate 22-part FBI “Jeffrey Epstein” case-file series. Both PDF page numbers and document stamps are recorded.

Three verified source findings

1. FBI official identified startup difficulties with DOJ assignments and Everlaw

January 5, 2026 email · PDF page 4 · FBI(26-cv-1832)-9. An FBI Information Management Division section chief reported that the day's Everlaw training was not sufficiently informative and that understanding how to perform redactions would take time. The same email described apparent confusion about how DOJ was assigning work, but also said FBI personnel were engaged and hoped the difficulties would improve during the week.

Evidence boundary: this establishes a contemporaneous internal concern, not proof of unlawful redactions, deliberate suppression or eventual noncompliance. Inspect original PDF page 4.

2. DOJ’s January 4 protocol specified review classifications

January 4, 2026 memorandum · PDF pages 6–8 · FBI(26-cv-1832)-11 through -13. The Office of the Deputy Attorney General instructed reviewers to assess responsiveness document by document, using Everlaw categories for responsive, non-responsive, duplicate, error and foreign language. The duplicate rule covered certain already published records and repeated email-chain content. The memo told reviewers to proceed to the next item without redacting documents marked duplicate or non-responsive.

Audit significance: a category defined in the protocol is not proof that any particular record was correctly classified. A versioned index and record-level comparison would be needed to assess omissions. See PDF pages 6–8.

3. Protocol tracked FD-302s, public officials and victim-protection rules

PDF pages 8–11 · FBI(26-cv-1832)-13 through -16. The memo called for separate tags for flight logs, agreements, charging decisions, destruction-related records and FBI FD-302 interview reports. It directed the Everlaw “Notes” box to identify government officials and politically exposed persons named or referenced in released records, while sending reviewer questions through designated channels. It also required redacting victim-identifying information, including surrounding context that might reveal identity.

These are written review instructions, not independent verification of each final redaction. GAH does not reproduce any victim-identifying material. Inspect PDF pages 8–11.

What the deleted-page sheet means—and does not mean

The FBI's FOIA/PA accounting sheet on PDF pages 1–2 lists 134 deleted pages with referral/consultation indications and exemption markings such as b6 and b7C. “Deleted page” is disclosure-processing terminology. The sheet does not prove investigative records were physically destroyed or intentionally concealed; legal grounds and withheld content require separate scrutiny.

Legal effect and outstanding questions

Legal effect: official FBI records disclosure. It is not a judicial determination, contempt finding, stay, finding of intentional wrongdoing or evidence that the underlying conduct occurred in October. The messages concern activity dated January 2026; their publication is October 2026.

  • Unresolved: when and whether the documented training/assignment uncertainty was addressed.
  • Unresolved: classification counts and the adequacy of quality-control procedures for duplicates and full redactions.
  • Next receipt: a record-level comparison of the review protocol against DOJ publication indexes, exemption explanations and any applicable court findings.
