17 State Attorneys General Back Epstein Files Transparency Act II

An October 15 multistate letter urges Congress to strengthen enforcement of Epstein-file disclosures and survivor access. The bills remain proposals.

Grok Archive Hub News Desk · Published October 20, 2026 · Primary event: October 15, 2026

A coalition of 17 state attorneys general supported passage of the Epstein Files Transparency Act II in a letter to congressional leaders dated October 15, 2026. Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes released the announcement that day; the Nevada Attorney General issued a further statement October 19 listing the participating states. This is a coalition's policy request, not a ruling or a new legal obligation.

What the coalition is requesting

The attorneys general support H.R. 9694 and S. 5005, introduced July 15, 2026. Their letter urges more explicit enforcement of existing Epstein disclosure requirements. Proposed changes include a federal cause of action and standing for specified state officials, survivors and members of Congress, rights for victims to access records about themselves, and limits on use of common-law privileges as a basis for withholding mandated material. The proposals also reference existing penalties for deliberate obstruction or destruction of covered records.

What the letter cannot establish

The coalition asserts that DOJ has not fully complied with the original transparency statute and raises concerns about victim privacy. Those are positions advanced by the attorneys general; the letter is not an independent court order, a verified inventory of all withheld files or a merits decision in Phang v. Blanche.

In the separate Phang docket, ECF 57 ordered redacted handwritten FBI notes submitted for judicial in-camera review by October 16, and ECF 59 contains the government's notice reporting that delivery. A party's notice is not itself judicial confirmation. The October 1 certified-list filing and a signed disposition of the ECF 51 deadline motion must still be independently established from reliable court records; no conclusion is drawn from rotating RSS or missing mirrors.

Current legal effect: none. Neither EFTA II bill has become law as of this source check on October 20, 2026. The 2025 Epstein Files Transparency Act remains a separate, enacted statute. The October 15 coalition letter cannot change a court deadline.

What GAH will verify next

Watch for amendments or signed legislation, then distinguish future standing or disclosure rights from proposals. Separately, preserve court-order versus litigant-filing labels in the Phang administrative-record tracker. Victim-identifying information must remain protected.
