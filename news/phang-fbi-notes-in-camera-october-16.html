Judge Orders Redacted FBI Notes for Private Review by October 16

A signed October 9 order requires three sets of handwritten FBI notes to be submitted to the judge. It does not require public release.

Grok Archive Hub News Desk · Published October 12, 2026 · About 2 minutes

Federal Judge Emmet G. Sullivan ordered the Justice Department to submit redacted versions of underlying FBI handwritten interview notes for private, in-camera review by noon Eastern on October 16, 2026. The order was signed October 9 in Phang v. Blanche, a case about Epstein-file disclosures.

The court had received unredacted handwritten notes for three FD-302 reports, identified as EFTA01245620, EFTA02858481 and EFTA02858491, but had not received the redacted versions it had requested. The new order requires the missing versions to be provided to the judge. It does not direct DOJ to release those notes to the public on October 16.

The ruling also resolved several separate issues in the government's favor. Judge Sullivan found adequate documentation for specified victim-name redactions and discharged related show-cause matters. He separately accepted an explanation for why the underlying handwritten notes associated with another report were not provided.

Questions about the certified administrative-record index and deadlines raised in other filings are separate from ECF 57. The September 16 order set October 1 and October 15 deadlines, but a current, authenticated disposition of the later extension request has not been established in this brief.

October 20 update: October 16 notice of compliance filed

DOJ filed ECF 59, its October 16 Notice of Compliance, reporting it hand-delivered the redacted handwritten notes for EFTA01245620, EFTA02858481 and EFTA02858491 to Judge Sullivan’s chambers at 10:55 a.m. Eastern, before the noon deadline in ECF 57. This establishes what DOJ reported in a court-stamped filing, not independent confirmation of the binder’s contents, approval of the redactions, or an order to release them publicly.

The government also requested a stay if the court later orders publication. That is not a stay already granted. Whether the certified administrative-record list was filed with the court October 1, whether the complete record was delivered to the plaintiff October 15, and whether a signed ruling granted ECF 51 remain separate, unresolved verification questions. See the full ECF 59 source report and docket-status table.

What remains unverified: ECF 59 supplies DOJ’s report of in-camera submission. Independent judicial confirmation, a signed decision on ECF 51, the October 1 certified-list filing and private October 15 record delivery have not been established in this brief.
