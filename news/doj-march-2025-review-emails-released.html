New DOJ Release Documents March 2025 Epstein File Review Instructions

A 41-page DOJ disclosure identifies instructions to flag public figures and protect victim identities. The approved review plan remains partly withheld.

Grok Archive Hub News Desk · Published October 12, 2026 · About 2 minutes

The Department of Justice released 41 pages of correspondence on October 9, 2026, showing how DOJ and FBI personnel organized a review of Epstein investigative files in March 2025. The records document instructions to flag additional public figures for contemporaneous review and discussions about safeguarding identifying information about victims.

A March 23 email refers to a starting spreadsheet of prominent names and tells reviewers to flag others they encounter. That is a direction to escalate names for review—not proof that their names were automatically redacted or that documents were improperly withheld.

Emails also describe victim-identification safeguards, training and processing questions. The categories are not interchangeable: a name mentioned in a source file does not establish wrongdoing, and a list used to protect victims and their relatives is not a roster of perpetrators.

The limits are significant. Portions of the approved March 22 project plan are withheld under a Freedom of Information Act exemption, and the referenced public-figure spreadsheet is not included with the release. The correspondence does not reveal how every individual review decision was made.

The October 9 publication was an interim FOIA disclosure, not a new court ruling. It provides part of the originating 2025 source chain, which predates the Epstein Files Transparency Act enacted later that year.

What remains unverified: Full approved review guidance, the attached spreadsheet, and outcomes for individual flagged names remain unverified.
