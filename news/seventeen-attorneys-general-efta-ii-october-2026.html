17 State Attorneys General Urge Congress to Advance Epstein Files Transparency Act II

A signed October 15 coalition letter backs H.R. 9694 and S. 5005 to expand enforcement of Epstein-records disclosure requirements and survivor access. The letter is advocacy, not a court order.

Grok Archive Hub News Desk · Published October 16, 2026 · 2-minute read

A five-page October 15, 2026 letter addressed to congressional leaders is signed by 17 state attorneys general, led by Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes. The signatories urge passage of the Epstein Files Transparency Act II, identified as H.R. 9694 and S. 5005. The bills were already introduced; the October 15 development is the multistate advocacy letter, not the enactment of a new statute.

According to the letter, the proposed measure would provide an express expedited federal cause of action for state attorneys general, other authorized officials, survivors and members of Congress to challenge wrongful withholding or excessive redaction of Epstein records. It would also protect survivor access to records about themselves and limit reliance on specified common-law privileges as grounds to avoid required disclosure.

The letter cites the June 25, 2026 opinion in Phang v. Blanche, D.D.C. No. 1:26-cv-01417, including the court's statement that the Attorney General conceded a violation of the original EFTA deadline. Its additional characterizations of the completeness of DOJ productions and the number of withheld records are the signatories' assertions, not new findings from an October 15 judicial ruling.

Legal status: H.R. 9694 and S. 5005 remain proposed legislation in the context of this October 15 letter. Sending the letter neither amends the original statute nor gives states or survivors new legal powers by itself. Whether, when or in what form Congress acts is unknown.

Evidence boundary: The signed letter is a primary record of state legal and political advocacy. It is not an order deciding Phang's pending motions; it does not establish that an October 1 certified record list is missing or that private record delivery failed. The number 17 comes from the signed letter, rather than inconsistent press-release summaries.
