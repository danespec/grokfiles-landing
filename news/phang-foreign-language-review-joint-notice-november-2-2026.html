DOJ Says Epstein Foreign-Language Review Has Begun; November 27 Deadline Disputed

DOJ now represents in a court filing that it has started reviewing records previously tagged as foreign-language documents under the Epstein Files Transparency Act. But the six-page joint notice does not establish when that work started, how many files are involved, or when any will be produced.

ECF 63 — November 2 Notice of Proposed Order, one-page PDF. This is not a signed judicial order. The separate proposed-order attachment was not independently reviewed.

1. What DOJ actually represented

In ECF 62, page 2, the government states that review has begun for documents already tagged as foreign-language records. It says it will evaluate responsiveness and then ensure that responsive documents are redacted in accordance with its attorney review protocol and EFTA.

DOJ recommends another update in 30 days, not a fixed production deadline. It argues that translation-related review challenges cannot be fully known in advance, rejects accusations of prior noncompliance and opposes questions about staffing and agency resource allocation.

Legal distinction: a government representation in a signed filing is primary evidence of what DOJ told the court. It is not independent proof of how much review occurred or any public production.

2. What Phang wants ordered

Phang's section of ECF 62, pages 2–4 proposes tighter judicial supervision. These demands are litigation requests, not orders currently established by this notice.

RequestRequested effect
November 27Complete foreign-language review within 30 days after the October 28 appellate ruling.
Rolling productionRelease responsive records DOJ finds subject to EFTA as they are identified, rather than wait for a final batch.
November 6 answersDisclose the tagged-document total, review start date and progress, personnel and labor hours, and first-level foreign-language review method.
Weekly FridaysProvide reviewed, remaining, responsive/produced, withheld by statutory ground, otherwise withheld and nonresponsive counts.
Delay noticeNotify court and plaintiff promptly if the proposed November 27 target cannot be met, including reasons and mitigation.

Phang also describes an unsuccessful effort to negotiate a shared plan. DOJ disputes her characterization of compliance. Joint filing does not mean joint agreement.

3. ECF 63 did not grant any request

ECF 63, filed November 2, is a one-page notice stating that plaintiff submits a proposed order for the court's consideration. No judicial signature or operative command appears in that publicly reviewed PDF. It cannot be characterized as adoption of the requested November 27 review deadline, November 6 baseline or Friday reports.

No subsequently signed order accepting those proposals was independently verified for this report. A partial RSS feed or mirror cannot establish that no additional filing exists.

5. What the documents leave unanswered

  • Start date, total volume and current completion level of the foreign-language review.
  • Any count of responsive records released, withheld or awaiting redaction.
  • Whether Judge Sullivan will approve or modify any of Phang's proposed dates and reports.
  • Whether the certified administrative-record list due October 1 under ECF 42 was filed with the court, and whether a signed order resolved the government's separate ECF 51 postponement motion. Missing public mirrors do not answer either question.
  • Whether judicial records later confirm or contradict DOJ's ECF 59 representation about its separate October 16 in-camera handwritten-notes delivery.

Neither courtroom silence nor the absence of a record in a rotating RSS feed proves a missed deadline or noncompliance.
