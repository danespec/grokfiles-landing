Epstein Records Appeal Accelerated: Court Sets March Argument Window as DOJ Files Record Submission

A signed appellate order sets an accelerated schedule for two consolidated appeals in the Epstein-records litigation and directs oral argument for the first appropriate date in March 2027. Separately, DOJ filed a district-court document captioned as a certified list of its administrative record. Neither new document grants a stay, decides the case's merits or orders immediate public disclosure.

1. What the appeals court changed

In Phang v. Blanche, consolidated appeals 26-5299 and 26-5334, the D.C. Circuit entered a per curiam order dated November 20 after considering a motion to expedite. It establishes the following briefing calendar and directs the clerk to calendar the cases for oral argument in March 2027. The exact date and the merits-panel composition will be announced later.

This is separate from the October 28 stay denial and the November 9 district-court decisions. It changes case scheduling, not the substantive disposition or the public accessibility of Epstein records.

3. What DOJ's November 17 certified-list filing establishes

On November 17, DOJ filed D.D.C. ECF 68, titled Defendant's Certified List of the Contents of the Administrative Record. DOJ states that it submits the certified list pursuant to the November 9 ECF 65 ruling, and represents that counsel met and conferred on November 16.

Important distinction: the authenticated public PDF contains the filing statement and certificate of service but does not itemize the administrative-record contents across its two pages. The filed document is verified; any separate detailed inventory, attachment or determination of completeness requires additional authoritative evidence. The two-page PDF alone does not establish which documents are included.

ECF 65 required the certified-list filing by November 17 and delivery of the underlying administrative record to the plaintiff by December 1. Filing a document with the court is distinct from delivering records to the plaintiff. The older October 1 list deadline cannot be treated as satisfied merely because ECF 68 was filed in November. ECF 65 already denied DOJ's ECF 51 postponement motion; that is an earlier ruling, not another new denial.

4. No automatic FBI-note release or noncompliance finding

The November 9 ECF 64 order separately directed publication of specified redacted handwritten FBI notes, subject to its conditional temporary-stay provision if DOJ timely sought both a stay and expedited appellate review. The new November 20 scheduling document does not itself decide whether this condition was triggered, whether notes were published or whether a deadline was met. Missing entries from rotating RSS feeds or partial docket mirrors are not proof of noncompliance.

As GAH previously reported, ECF 64 says the district judge reviewed the notes in camera, independently confirming judicial review. That is distinct from making the redacted notes public.
