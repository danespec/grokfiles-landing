Epstein Wall Street Records Act: What H.R. 10820 Would Require

Congress introduced a proposal to open bank regulators' Epstein-related supervisory records, require investigations and protect victim privacy. It is not law.

Grok Archive Hub News Desk · Published October 20, 2026 · Primary event: October 15, 2026

On October 15, 2026, lawmakers introduced the Epstein Wall Street Records Act of 2026. The official introduced House text is H.R. 10820, referred to the House Financial Services Committee. Senator Elizabeth Warren lists the Senate measure, S. 5685, as introduced on the same date and referred to the Senate Banking Committee. This is a proposed law, not an enacted disclosure mandate.

What the introduced House bill actually requires

Records: Section 2(b) would require the Federal Reserve, OCC, FDIC and SEC to provide covered supervisory records to designated congressional committee leaders within 90 days after enactment and publish searchable, downloadable records within 120 days after enactment, with victim-identifying information and victim medical/personal files withheld or redacted. Agencies would have to explain each withholding or redaction.

Investigations: Section 2(c) would direct financial regulators to investigate covered institutions and senior executives, including possible banking, securities and anti-money-laundering violations, compliance controls, transaction values and document destruction. Section 2(f) would require a congressional findings report within 180 days after enactment.

Potential sanctions: The introduced bill includes industry bars and civil penalties after specified agency findings, with notice and an opportunity for hearing. It also requires criminal referrals when regulators reasonably find that a federal criminal violation may have occurred. It does not itself establish that any institution or executive committed an offense.

What the launch announcement adds—and does not prove

Senate Banking Committee minority members published the bill announcement with regulator and bank correspondence, including responses labeled for the OCC, FDIC, Federal Reserve, Barclays, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan Chase. These records are available for comparison to previous Senate Finance Committee research; the lawmakers' characterizations are advocacy and oversight claims, not adjudicated findings.

Current legal effect: none. Introduction and committee referral do not start any 90-, 120- or 180-day clock. The potential scope is broad, but the final text, if any, could change. Association with Epstein does not establish criminal misconduct.

The open verification trail

GAH will watch for committee action, amendments, enactment, regulator production, and source-level joins between any released examination records and the existing financial-document archive. Until actual documents appear, no new banking investigation or violation is asserted here.
