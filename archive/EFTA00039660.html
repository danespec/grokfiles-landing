Reading the three labels without collapsing their dates

The useful distinction on these renders is between three different administrative fields. A suicide-watch entry, a psychological-observation entry and a pending SHU-bedspace entry describe different categories. Reading them as one sentence would erase both their labels and the person attached to each line. The visible name Lopez belongs to the bedspace entry identified above. Moving that name or its status onto the Epstein observation row would change the source rather than explain it.

Compare page 459 with the continuation marked by the EFTA00039661 footer before quoting a row. The difference between the PDF artifact identifier and the rendered footer is documented here so that a reader can locate the same material without assuming that two filenames necessarily mean two independent accounts. Neither image, on its own, supplies a continuous custody history.

The chronology also has a stopping point. These late-July pages cannot establish whether an observation designation continued, ended or changed before August 9. To connect the snapshot to the final night, a later dated status sheet would need to identify the same inmate and the relevant category. Until such a record is attached, a timeline should preserve the July date and leave the intervening change unresolved. This is a reason to keep the row available for inspection, rather than to turn it into a verdict about later supervision.
