## The disclosure-reconciliation question: 2016 and 2017 Part VIII say “NONE”

**2016:** The original 990-PF, **PDF page 7** (printed Form page 7), Part VIII line 3, says **“NONE”** in the table for five highest-paid independent contractors for professional services. This is against the same year's **$290,000** consulting-expense schedule.

**2017:** The original 2017 990-PF, **PDF page 7**, likewise lists **“NONE”** and a total of zero other contractors over $50,000 for professional services, against **$235,000** reported consulting expenses.

The **contemporaneous official IRS 2016 Form 990-PF instructions**, printed p. 28 / PDF page 28, explain that Part VIII line 3 requires the five highest-paid **independent contractors paid more than $50,000 for personal services of a professional nature**, including professional service firms. The threshold is **strictly greater than $50,000**, not greater-than-or-equal.

- [Official 2016 IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/i990pf--2016.pdf), “Line 3. Five highest-paid independent contractors for professional services.”

### Why this is not yet proof of an inaccurate IRS filing

The annual consultation total could be shared across multiple payees; it may also include services not classified as “personal services of a professional nature” for this return. **A separately reported advisory-board consulting contract and a Form 1099 could be important**, but precise status and amounts need the original contracting and accounting records. Nothing now establishes that the “NONE” line was knowingly false or that payments were structured to avoid reporting thresholds.

## 2017 and 2018: separate payee audits

2017's reported **$235,000** consulting category and 2018's **$20,000** consulting category are supported by their year-specific filings. Third-party indexes identify possible consulting-related payments or proposals involving Noam Chomsky and others in 2017, but this investigation did not authenticate each original check, agreement or tax booking. They are **leads, not an allocated recipient ledger**.

A separate possibility arises from the 2017 Form 990-PF grant schedule: it lists **$375,000 paid to the International Peace Institute**, and a contemporaneous institutional review reportedly itemizes receipts from the same foundation. That is **grant spending, not consulting fees**. Do not merge grant reports with consultant payments or turn a foundation grant into proof of wrongdoing by recipients.
