Evidence groups and limits

  • MCC scene (23). Scene views can describe layout. They cannot reconstruct the unrecorded interval.
  • Autopsy examination (102). The signed report and interview supply the posterior-neck discrepancy. Photographic appearances alone cannot date an injury.
  • Forensic anthropology (43). Structural findings require specialist interpretation alongside the examination record.
  • Neuropathology (18). Gross photographs alone cannot establish a microscopic diagnosis.
  • Evidence and packaging (29). Labels may aid identification. Chain of custody requires independent records.

Publication decision

The archive preserves the complete sequence so independent reviewers can compare adjacent frames and evaluate whether selected images omit relevant context. The JPEGs support comparison by page. The PDFs preserve pagination for reference and offline review. The collection is documentary, and both full-resolution formats may substitute for the secondary compilation. Ownership and the licensing market remain unresolved.

Source hashes identify bytes. They do not authenticate who took a photograph or when.

How to interpret the images

Photographs document appearances, not automatically when injuries occurred or what caused them. The images do not establish homicide, substitution, or an ordered killing. The best-supported explanation remains suicide, alongside documented custodial failures and unresolved forensic-record discrepancies. Read the source-grounded analysis before making claims from any isolated photograph.
